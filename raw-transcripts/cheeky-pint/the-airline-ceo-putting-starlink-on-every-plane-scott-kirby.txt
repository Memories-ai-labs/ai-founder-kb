## Episode Summary

Scott Kirby, CEO of United Airlines, joins Cheeky Pint hosts John Collison and Ben Thompson to discuss fleet strategy, airline technology, Starlink deployment, and Kirby's unconventional management philosophy.

## Key Points

**Contrarian fleet bet during COVID:**
- While other global long-haul carriers shrank during COVID, United placed a huge aircraft order
- Kirby's logic: "we don't have to be right. We just have to be not totally wrong."
- Contrarian capital allocation when competitors retreat — classic optionality move

**Skepticism of surveys:**
- Uses surveys only to understand how customers felt about *past* changes, not to decide future moves
- Prefers to ship quick experiments and keep what works
- Customer surveys are poor predictors of the future

**Technology as differentiator:**
- United moved off mainframes, owns its reservation system, and has brought more systems in-house
- AI useful for building tools and forecasting (e.g., modeling Denver's pop-up thunderstorms to reduce weather delays)
- Founder-like ownership: in-house software means faster iteration than outsourcing to vendors

**Starlink deployment:**
- ~48% of United's flights had Starlink at the time of recording, targeting 50%
- Speed (not free service) is the key value proposition
- Advantage will be temporary as competitors catch up — acknowledges moat is time-limited

**Network discipline:**
- United focuses on its hubs and "catchment-area" flying
- Cuts routes that lose money, experiments with new destinations (e.g., Nuuk), closes what doesn't work (e.g., Bergen)
- Discipline over growth: willingness to kill things that underperform

**Air traffic control (ATC):**
- Biggest constraint on the industry
- Slow government processes and local politics block modernization
- External bottleneck that airlines can't control

**Competition and costs:**
- High airport fees have priced low-cost carriers out of major markets
- Spirit failed because its fares didn't cover costs
- Ryanair works because of its cost structure discipline in Europe

**Airframes and supply chain:**
- Boeing and Airbus have limited incentive to develop new aircraft
- United invested in JetZero (blended-wing-body developer) to create competitive pressure
- Calls for a third aircraft manufacturer

**Culture:**
- Creating "wow" moments for customers
- Avoiding weather as an excuse for delays
- Empowering teams to act without layers of approval
- Kirby does weekend vibe-coding projects (engineering background signals)
