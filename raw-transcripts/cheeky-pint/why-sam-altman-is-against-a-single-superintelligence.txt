MAIN TOPICS:

Cybersecurity:
AI models are getting much better at finding software vulnerabilities. Altman expects more serious flaws to surface as people scan more code. Fixing software alone isn't enough — architectures may need to change. Startups are exploring new stacks for routers, operating systems, and applications.

Safety and sandboxing:
Altman is skeptical that sandboxing alone can contain very capable models. Monitoring and high confidence in alignment would also be needed. Defends iterative deployment — the public needs a clear case for why benefits outweigh risks.

Agent teams vs. swarms:
Altman prefers "agent teams" to "swarms." Many interacting agents might resemble the resilience of pluralistic society better than a single centralized system. (Hence the title: against a single superintelligence — distributed intelligence is safer.)

Compute and math:
OpenAI spent heavily on compute for research including a Navier-Stokes proof. Tension between publishing fundamental discoveries and respecting working mathematicians. Memory and data center constraints are severe. Market response has been slow. OpenAI working on own chips and robotics for data center construction.

Personal agents and commerce:
No personal agent has yet reached a "Macintosh moment." Businesses may need to adapt as personal AI agents replace web forms and mobile apps. Questions whether businesses will accept AI agents acting on their behalf or keep their own apps and interfaces.

Other: Benchmark gaming, model "personality," token-based pricing, Greg Brockman.
