SHOW NOTES / KEY INSIGHTS:

Josh Woodward is VP at Google Labs, where he runs the Gemini app, AI Studio, NotebookLM, and Flow. He joined Lenny Rachitsky to talk about how Labs finds, tests, and kills AI product ideas — and why corporate labs usually die within three or four years.

Key points surfaced in the episode:

1. OPPORTUNITY INVENTORY: Labs keeps a running list of things that are "nearly possible." They act as soon as a model makes an idea technically viable, rather than waiting for scheduled innovation sprints.

2. TEAM SIZE COMPRESSION: New lab projects now start with 2–3 people, down from 5–7 a few years ago. Building products is cheaper, so the bar for starting is lower and the bar for sustaining is higher.

3. IDEA SOURCING: Strong ideas come from ongoing experimentation, not brainstorms. The design-sprint model doesn't match the pace at which model capabilities evolve.

4. EARLY PMF SIGNALS: Woodward looks for user reaction quality, not just daily active users or retention curves. Early emotional response is a leading indicator.

5. KILLING PROJECTS: Teams often see the end coming before leadership does. Woodward says the best lab leaders recognize this signal and act on it early.

6. HIRING HEURISTIC: He looks for how quickly candidates abandon ideas that testing proves wrong. Fast updating is more valuable than strong conviction.

7. WHY LABS FAIL: Most corporate labs fade within three to four years. Woodward argues the core requirement is structural independence from existing business units, plus clear mandates to convert experiments into products.

8. BENCHMARKS ARE OVERHYPED: Model benchmark rankings don't reflect real user value. Most users choose products by feel, not Elo score.

Products shipped from Labs: NotebookLM (now large user base), AI Studio (developer platform), Flow (video generation), and the Gemini app itself.
