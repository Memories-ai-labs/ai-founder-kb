## Overview

Elizabeth Stone is the Chief Product and Technology Officer (CPTO) at Netflix. Previously VP of Science at Lyft, COO at Nuna, economist at Analysis Group, and trader at Merrill Lynch. The conversation explores how Netflix thinks about roles, talent, and excellence in the AI era.

## Key Topics

### AI's Role Confusion
AI has created confusion about professional identity — "everyone can be everything now. PMs can ship code, designers can write PRDs, engineers can product." Elizabeth frames this as a natural "storming phase before forming phase" in technology transitions.

### Systems Thinkers vs. Specialists
Netflix is hiring for more **systems thinkers** — people who can abstract across business domains to identify necessary infrastructure — rather than narrow specialists.
- "We need more systems thinkers in a world with AI...people who can look across all the business domains and abstract that to, here's the building blocks we're going to need."
- Design teams are evolving toward design systems and templates rather than individual feature design.

### Craft Mastery Still Scarce
Despite AI, "I still see a craft excellence that's really important...I still find great engineering to be scarce, great data science to be scarce, great creativity to be scarce."

### AI Fluency as Universal Expectation
Netflix hasn't made AI skills level-specific on career ladders. Instead, "AI fluency" is a universal expectation across all roles and seniority levels.

### Beyond Coding — AI Use Cases at Netflix
1. Data Analysis: distilling research and consumer insights across Netflix's history
2. Content Production: pre-visualization, relighting, reframing, dialogue changes in post-production (Netflix acquired Interpositive)
3. Localization & Asset Creation: subtitles, dubs, trailers, and artwork at scale

### Excellence as an Operating System
Core pillars:
1. **Talent Density**: "the non-negotiable"
2. **Comfort with Risk-Taking**: Netflix doesn't avoid failures; it recovers quickly
3. **Clear Outcomes**: decisions must drive value for consumers and Netflix
4. **Resistance to Process**: adding process "actually goes against...better outcomes"
5. **Trust in Decision-Making**: leaders must resist overruling decisions that aren't material

### The Keeper's Test
"Would I hire this person today, knowing what I know? Or: If they left, would I fight to keep them?" — operates positively most of the time.

### Junior Talent
Netflix continues hiring interns and new graduates — fresh perspectives and cultural fluency remain essential, but craft mastery is still required.
- "Mastery of the craft is still very important...I am responsible for the quality of code that I am submitting for production."

### Entertainment's Future
Netflix sees entertainment expanding beyond film/TV to games, live content, podcasts, and interactive formats.
- "The future of entertainment isn't going to be one thing and it's going to have to be more personalized, more immersive, more interactive."
