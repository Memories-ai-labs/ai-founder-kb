## Segment 1: Building an iPhone App with Zero Technical Skills
**Guest:** Bryce Rattner Keithley (talent/recruiting professional, no coding background)

Built and shipped Daily Hundred, a fitness app featuring AI-generated workout videos of anthropomorphic animals, to the App Store.

**Tools used:** Claude, Claude Code, Replit, Gemini, Higgsfield, Kling

**Workflow:**
1. Planning with Claude (architect)
2. Executing with Claude Code (engineer)
3. Deploying via Terminal

Key insight: "You can build and ship a production iPhone app with zero technical background" — technical expertise requirements are shifting; hiring for adaptability over specialized credentials is now viable.

## Segment 2: The Codex Feature That Works While You Sleep
**Guest:** Claire Vo

Codex's /goal feature enables autonomous AI agents to work on multi-step tasks for extended periods without constant supervision.

**Six-component framework for effective Goals:**
1. Outcome
2. Verification
3. Constraints
4. Boundaries
5. Iteration policy
6. Stopping conditions

Claire reported successful runs lasting nearly six hours. Goals transform AI "from a tool you have to constantly babysit into an agent that can work for hours on multi-step tasks."

Real-world applications: error remediation, email management, task organization.

## Segment 3: Claude Opus 4.8 Review
**Reviewer:** Claire Vo

Comprehensive evaluation of Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 model tested across coding, design, and strategy work vs. Opus 4.7.

**Strengths:**
- Voice quality and ergonomics
- Token efficiency
- Greenfield prototyping
- New agentic features: dynamic workflows and effort controls

**Weaknesses (significant):**
- **Hallucinations when stuck** — "The model hallucinates when it gets stuck" — notable regression from recent standards
- Poor performance in existing codebases
- Limited ambition for complex tasks
- Opus 4.7 excels at business strategy; Opus 4.8 struggles with numerical analysis
- Production readiness concerns — testing requirements are significant

Overall verdict: promising on new projects but problematic for production environments where hallucination tolerance is low.
