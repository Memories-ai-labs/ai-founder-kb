## Guest
Evan Conrad, CEO of SF Compute

## Summary

SF Compute operates a physical compute exchange — buyers receive actual usable GPU capacity, not financial derivatives. The episode covers the design of their order book, compute market liquidity, hardware cohort pricing, and how physical settlement shapes their market structure.

## Key Points

### Origin
- SF Compute began by trying to sublease a GPU cluster
- Customer needs pushed the company to build much of a cloud's operating capacity to reliably deliver traded compute

### Physical Settlement Model
- Buyers receive usable compute, not a cash payment tied to an index
- Key differentiator: prevents market manipulation that plagues cash-settled compute indices
- Factors affecting contract terms: interruptibility, storage recovery, GPU generation, networking
- Analogy: delivery constraints in oil and power markets

### Liquidity
- ~5% of total capacity on the order book; volume growing
- Practical measure: a buyer spending $100 can recoup ~$25 by reselling capacity
- Liquidity defined by whether it solves customers' problems, not theoretical depth

### Order Book Design
- Matches orders on multiple attributes simultaneously rather than waiting for standardized grades
- Enables trading during fast technology change
- Trade-off: wider spreads and more fragmented liquidity vs. standardized contracts

### Term Structure and Pricing
- Clusters typically financed over 3–5 years; capacity blocks tradeable up to multi-year terms with upfront payment
- Forward curve rises and falls depending on timing and buyer profile
- Gap between B300 inference revenue and rental costs suggests price adjustment ahead

### Hardware Cohorts
- Early H100 and GB200 units had defects and firmware issues
- Buyers may pay premium for later production batches — hardware quality signal matters

### Independence
- SF Compute does not own hardware — preserves neutrality and avoids manipulating the forwards market
- Views cash-settled compute products as useful only when derived from physical settlement
- Warns: thin index data can be manipulated

### Operations and Partnerships
- Work includes site visits, cluster design, rack rollout, and backplane management
- Partners with data center operators and sovereign compute projects
- Feature: customers can subdivide InfiniBand fabrics on the fly
- Traded volume: several hundred million dollars

## Significance
SF Compute represents a new infrastructure layer — compute as a tradeable, physically-settled commodity. If this market structure scales, it could significantly change how AI companies provision and plan GPU capacity.
