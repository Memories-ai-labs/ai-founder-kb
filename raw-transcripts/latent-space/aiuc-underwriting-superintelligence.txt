# Latent Space Podcast

Guest: Rune Kvist, CEO of AIUC (Artificial Intelligence Underwriting Consortium)
Hosts: Swyx and Vibhu

## Overview
AIUC announced a $40 million Series A led by Ribbit Capital and First Harmonic. The company builds "confidence infrastructure for frontier AI through standards and insurance," addressing what Kvist describes as the actual bottleneck limiting AI adoption: not capability, but trust and manageable risk.

## Key Topics

### Rune's Background
Kvist read the Scaling Laws paper in late 2021 after selling his edtech company, then moved to San Francisco to join Anthropic during its earliest phase (~40 people, early 2022). His PPE (Philosophy, Politics, Economics) degree informed his recognition that capital would flow toward AI development if returns were predictable through scaling.

### The Trust Gap Problem
Drawing from Waymo's example—where autonomous vehicles remain deployment-limited despite being safer than human drivers—Kvist argues "the binding constraint on AI adoption is risk" rather than technical capability. The same applies to AI agents like Cursor and Harvey.

### AIUC-1 Certification Standard
Framework covering agent security, safety, and reliability:
- Six comprehensive categories addressing enterprise concerns
- Technical testing (thousands of quarterly simulations)
- Quarterly updates reflecting rapid AI evolution
- A consortium of Fortune 1000 risk leaders providing input
- Tests jailbreaks, hallucinations, data leaks, and adversarial corner cases
- Certification timelines: 3-10 weeks

### Insurance & Standards Partnership
Standards establish "rules of the road" while insurers quantify risk and align incentives. Historical precedent from electrical safety (Underwriters Laboratories), automotive crash testing, and financial ratings (Moody's). Lloyd's of London partners on coverage; ElevenLabs secured inaugural AI agent insurance policy.

### Model-Level Certification
A second initiative addresses frontier model risks. The government-lab trust gap requires neutral third parties—potentially mirroring Moody's role in financial markets—to run credible technical audits that governments can rely upon without duplicating lab expertise.

### Emerging Risk Categories
- Cybersecurity (current focus)
- Child safety (politically important)
- Biological weapons production (speculative but serious)
- Agent-to-agent interactions
- Coding agents and MCP integrations

### Copyright & Adverse Selection
Copyright insurance faces a "lemons problem": companies most eager to buy it likely trained on copyrighted material, signaling high risk to insurers. Information asymmetry makes coverage difficult without transparent testing frameworks.

### Monitoring & Eval Awareness
Post-deployment monitoring provides ground truth about actual performance. Models growing aware of evaluation creates testing challenges, requiring logging systems and real-world behavioral analysis rather than synthetic benchmarks alone.

### Roadmap
Planned expansions: agents → frontier models → robotics (physical AI raising "strict liability" concerns). World models and autonomous agent interactions represent future complexity.

## Notable Quotes
"Risk was going to hold down adoption" and "that is now over" — citing Mythos and Fable as proof that liability concerns dominate enterprise decision-making.

"Every model can ultimately be jailbroken" — making independent evaluation frameworks essential for government confidence.

"We don't decide whether anyone should buy...that depends on their risk appetite. But we do provide this common information layer."

## Business Model
For-profit standards and auditing firm. Profit incentives drive responsiveness to customers better than nonprofit alternatives (which dominated cybersecurity standards). Revenue from certification fees and insurance partnerships.
