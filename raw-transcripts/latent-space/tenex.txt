Context: Tenex is an applied AI partner helping large enterprises move from "AI absent to AI native." ~75 people, split roughly evenly between Fortune 2000 and mid-market clients.

Key topics:

Paying for output: Tenex uses story points for compensation. Feared problems (sandbagging estimates, shipping bad code for points) haven't materialized, partly because AI strategists are incentivized on customer retention. Harder challenge: compensating non-coding work like architecture and client discovery. Output per engineer roughly doubled over the past year; point values recalibrated quarterly.

MetaHarness: A shared set of skills, documentation, and a CLI that structures agent work. Plans, decisions, and logs are checked into Git so context stays in the repo rather than in people's heads.

Models and costs: Opus 5.5 is the team's default driver, with Codex used for review. Token spend is roughly 1–2x employee spend. Falling per-task costs make runaway token spending less of a concern than 6–8 months ago.

Enterprise adoption gap: Most enterprises have rolled out a general-purpose LLM, but far fewer have trained staff, deployed coding agents broadly, or put agents into production for non-engineering work. Permissioning and access control for AI tools is a major CIO/CISO pain point.

Kill My SaaS: Experiment where builders replaced a speaker-management tool quoted at ~$40K/year. Discussion covered whether replacing mid-tier software is worth the maintenance burden, and whether owning the workflow offers benefits beyond cost.

Vertical AI: Alex is generally skeptical of vertical AI products because of margin and one-of-one expectations. CJ and the host push back, citing domain data, specialized interfaces, and workflow habits as durable advantages.

AI and jobs: Reallocation rather than pure displacement; role of individual agency in reskilling.

Key line: "You can't automate a process that does not exist."
