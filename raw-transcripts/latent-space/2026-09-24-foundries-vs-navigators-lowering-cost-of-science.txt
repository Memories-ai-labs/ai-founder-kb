# Foundries vs Navigators: Lowering the Cost of Science

## Core Thesis (by Adrian Sanborn)

AI has made scientific thinking cheap but hasn't reduced experimental costs, creating two adaptive strategies in biotech:

1. **Foundries**: Industrialize measurement through technology like next-generation sequencing and high-throughput microscopy, making data generation faster
2. **Navigators**: Leverage AI to improve decision-making and workflows within existing operations

## Key Operational Impacts

### Flexible Analysis
Researchers now modify experimental protocols without rebuilding analysis pipelines, enabling agility in scientific discovery.

### Custom Tools
Teams build proprietary data dashboards and portals in hours rather than months, embedding their own decision-making logic.

### Scaled Expertise
Endura used language model agents to evaluate 500 disease targets where human review would have taken centuries of expert time.

## The Navigation Advantage

Navigators remain "invisible" because gains are operational rather than flashy. However, they're accessible to all research organizations, not just well-capitalized ones with specialized infrastructure. Early-stage startups particularly benefit since they lack legacy constraints.

## Unresolved Challenge

Reproducibility concerns when analyses are generated on-demand. Faster science requires trustworthy documentation of thinking processes alongside measurements.

## Founder Takeaways

- The "cost of thinking" has been commoditized; "cost of doing" remains high
- Distribution matters: navigators can scale expertise without proportional headcount growth
- Early-stage advantage: no legacy systems to retool means faster AI adoption
