## Summary

Vinoo Ganesh explores the often-misunderstood role of Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs), drawing from his experience building Palantir's Project Frontline program, which trained approximately 250 software engineers for field deployment.

## Key Distinctions

FDEs are often confused with sales engineers, consultants, or quota-carrying representatives. The term has been co-opted to mean "something close to a person who does something that vaguely involves a customer," creating significant role ambiguity.

The true purpose of an FDE is to understand a customer's operational model by learning their "nouns and verbs" — the terminology and processes embedded within their business. These unwritten systems represent how organizations actually function, often residing in spreadsheets or institutional memory rather than documentation.

## Critical Principle: Platform Over Services

Solving problems locally creates consulting work; translating field insights into generalizable platform capabilities creates lasting value.

Cautionary example: "vinoo.groovy" — a temporary script that became a year-long technical debt because it was never formalized into the product.

## Organizational Positioning

Where FDEs report determines their incentives:
- Sales-aligned teams: optimize for closing individual accounts
- Product-aligned teams: optimize for insights that improve the platform for future deployments

## Competitive Advantage

Rather than protecting proprietary models or talent, sustainable competitive advantage comes from "accumulated, current, verified understanding of how firms in a vertical actually operate," embedded within platforms that remain current and provable.

## Relevance for AI Founders

The FDE model is particularly relevant as AI companies deploy AI systems into enterprise workflows — the role of translating raw AI capabilities into operational fit for specific industries requires this pattern: learn the customer's vocabulary, translate to platform, never stay as bespoke code.
