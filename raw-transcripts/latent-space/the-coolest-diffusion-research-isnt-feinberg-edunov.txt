## Episode Summary

The episode explores how diffusion models are revolutionizing protein-ligand binding prediction rather than language models. Sergey Edunov, formerly lead of Llama training at Meta, joined Genesis Molecular AI after discovering their innovative architectural work in 3D structure prediction.

## Key Discussion Points

**The Scale Challenge**
"There are 10 to the 60 drug-like small molecules in the universe… it's like finding a needle in a haystack, where everything except your needle is very, very dangerous."

**Benchmark Critique**
Evan Feinberg argues the academic community's 2-angstrom RMSD threshold is misleading. He contends 1-angstrom accuracy is necessary for reliable molecular interaction modeling, particularly for hydrogen bonding which operates within 0.6-angstrom ranges.

**PEARL Model**
Genesis's PEARL model achieved zero-shot success on OpenBind benchmarks without fine-tuning, modeling protein flexibility and ligand-induced fit without lengthy simulations.

**Agentic Discovery**
Their internal system (SAPPHIRE) enables autonomous drug discovery loops through iterative hypothesis testing and laboratory partnerships with automated testing facilities.

**Why diffusion beats LLMs here**
The 3D geometry of molecular binding is fundamentally different from sequence prediction — diffusion models that operate in continuous 3D space are better suited than autoregressive token models for capturing how molecules physically interact.

**Sergey Edunov's move from Meta/Llama**
Former head of Llama training left Meta to join Genesis, reflecting the growing view among top ML researchers that bio/science applications represent the next frontier for large-scale model development.
