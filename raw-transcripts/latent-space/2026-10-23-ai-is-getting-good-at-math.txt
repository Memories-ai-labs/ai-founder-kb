## Core Question

Recent AI math breakthroughs include: OpenAI counterexample to Erdős's unit distance conjecture, Cycle Double Cover proof, Navier-Stokes proof; Anthropic work on Fermat's Last Theorem and Riemann hypothesis bounds. The article asks: what makes a math problem tractable for an LLM, and can we predict which problems models will solve next?

## Framework: Problem Difficulty as Insight Sequences

Following Gowers: difficulty = shortest hint sequence that lets experts reconstruct a proof. Formula: 2^k + t (k = number of hints, t = work to develop them). Complexity grows exponentially with insight count.

## Human vs. LLM Search

Human search is breadth-first: explore directions shallowly, abandon early. Testing a direction takes months — need signs of promise before committing.

LLM search is depth-first: produce one trajectory at a time, parallel agents multiply attempts. Compute substitutes for human time. Automated verifier prunes finished attempts → spending on uncertain ideas becomes rational. (Verifier only judges completed arguments, not half-formed ones.)

## The Harness Architecture

Three roles:
1. Drafter — writes prompts informed by past failures (negative hints)
2. Solver — runs parallel candidate paths
3. Verifier — checks validity

Failures become hints: OpenAI CDC prompt lists known failure modes as standing checks, tells model to assume proof exists, rules out substitute results.

## Case Studies

Unit-distance conjecture (Gowers' 3 hints): look for counterexample, generalize best known construction, try number fields of increasing degree. Mathematician Jacob Tsimerman considered the counterexample direction but stopped — found search too unpromising. Insight problems give no sense of progress until solved (psychology research cited).

Navier-Stokes: Built on prior work by Córdoba and Martínez-Zoroa. Fewer conceptual leaps than its 166-page length suggests. ~10,000 agents, ~300B tokens, 88 hours.

## Three Capability Gains This Year

1. Better at chaining primitives (lowering effort t)
2. Better at self-verification within a trajectory (cheaper pruning)
3. Better at orchestrating subagents and memory (negative hints accumulate)

## Branching Factor

If each step has b plausible next hints, k hints = b^k paths. Models succeed when b^k × compute cost stays manageable. Cybersecurity was early success: low branching factor. Labs may favor problems with narrow solution spaces.

## Human-AI Complement

Humans shrink branches using taste/judgment. Models shrink time to explore one branch. Problems with many insights (large k) or high branching (large b) remain hard for both. Gowers expects human intuition to matter most there.

Failures carry knowledge: human failures are informative because experts commit months based on judgment. AI failures are cheap and mostly naive, rarely shared. Terence Tao: announcing only final solutions confuses destination with journey.

## Speculative: General Relativity

Author suggests a model trained on data to 1901 might eventually find GR — the key hint (gravity as geometry) narrows the search. Einstein took ~8 years from key insight to full theory; failed attempts (gravity as force, field in flat space, tweak to Newton) would gradually make geometric idea more likely.
