## Overview

This episode explores how Genomic Language Models (GLMs) present both opportunities and risks in biological research. Radical Numerics applies techniques like chain-of-thought reasoning and multimodal perception to advance biological capabilities while addressing biosecurity challenges.

## Background on GLMs

Eric previously contributed to developing Evo and Evo 2 at Arc Institute. These models enabled a separate team to "generate entire bacteriophage genomes that were synthesized into functional viruses." Early skepticism from biologists regarding GLM viability eventually gave way to practical applications.

## DNA as a Language

Unlike natural language, DNA uses a four-character alphabet (ACTG) with extremely long sequences:
- Average human genes: 60,000 characters
- Longest sequences: up to 2.3 million characters
- Complete human genome: ~3 billion characters

Long-context model innovations enabled processing these scales approximately three years before frontier labs developed million-token contexts.

## Chain-of-Thought in Biology

The models demonstrate progressive reasoning by learning RNA aptamer patterns. When shown lower-performing examples ranked sequentially, the model extrapolates to predict higher-scoring variants it hasn't encountered.

## Central Argument

Nguyen contends that defensive biosecurity capabilities must advance alongside offensive AI capabilities to maintain parity in what he frames as an evolving technological arms race.
