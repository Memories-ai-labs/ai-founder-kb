# Building AI for Reliable Execution: Lessons From Industrial Robotics
# URL: https://www.latent.space/p/standard-bots
# Date: 2026-10-10
# Source: Latent Space (article by Richard MacManus)
# Guests: Evan Beard (co-founder & CEO, Standard Bots), Leif Jentoft (Head of AI, Standard Bots)

## Business context

- Standard Bots builds industrial robot arms (not humanoids) for machine tending, welding, assembly.
- Raised $200M Series C at $1B valuation. Customers: NASA, Amazon, Lockheed Martin.

## Model strategy

- Shared base model adapted per customer via demonstrations and fine-tuning.
- Multiple models: includes a zero-shot perception system for machine tending.
- Largest model has low-billions of parameters — far smaller than frontier models.
- Prioritizes data quality and targeted data over raw dataset size. Real-world interventions fix edge cases with ~dozens of examples.

## Division of labor

- Learned models handle perception (locating/identifying parts); conventional programming handles motion and cell logic.
- Focus on short-horizon tasks to meet cycle-time and reliability requirements.
- Simulation used where possible, but liquids, suction, and flexible material cutting are hard to simulate accurately.

## Full-stack co-optimization

- Controls arm, end effector, control system, and AI → can co-optimize models and control policies.
- Jentoft: no model is truly hardware-agnostic; tight coupling between low-level control and higher-level functions improves performance and iteration speed.

## Deployment architecture

- Training: cloud. Inference: on-premises on edge GPUs.
- Sensor data flows over internal gigabit Ethernet; GPUs output "action chunks" to low-level control.
- Local inference critical — many factories lack reliable internet; uptime drives customer acceptance.

## Fleet learning

- Captures failure signals and human corrections from deployments.
- Air-gapped defense customers keep data local; others contribute data in exchange for performance gains.

## Developer platform

- StandardOS: APIs and SDKs so developers can use parts of the stack or bring their own models (e.g., NVIDIA Cosmos).
- Plans to simplify data collection, training, and deployment.

## Key takeaway

- Keep the model's role narrow, use real-world corrections for edge cases, invest in targeted data. Applies to software agents too.
