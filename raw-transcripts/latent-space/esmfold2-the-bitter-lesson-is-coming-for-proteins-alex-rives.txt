## Key Announcement

Alex Rives announced ESMFold2, a new open scientific tool for protein prediction, design, and discovery. The system demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on protein interactions, particularly antibodies used in therapeutics.

## The Scaling Approach vs. Specialized Design

The ESM team rejected the specialized inductive biases of AlphaFold2 (which relied on multi-sequence alignments) in favor of training massive transformer models on diverse protein sequence data. This approach mirrors the scaling philosophy proven effective in large language models — "the bitter lesson": general methods that leverage computation tend to win over methods that encode domain knowledge.

## World Model Architecture

ESMFold2 implements a two-part structure:
- **ESMC**: A world model trained on 2.8 billion protein sequences
- **ESMFold2**: A structure-prediction component built on top

This enables downstream applications like property prediction, functional decomposition, and design searches.

## Programmable Biology Vision

The article frames cells as computational systems where genes function as programs, ribosomes as compilers, and proteins as processes executing biological workflows. By extracting semantic features from the model using techniques like Sparse Auto Encoders, researchers can identify conceptual patterns (membrane integration, disordered regions, disulfide bonds) that compose into novel protein designs.

## Resources Released

An atlas of 6.8 billion proteins and 1.1 billion predicted structures, released under MIT license.
