## Guest Background

Alex Zhang is an MIT doctoral researcher known for work on Recursive Language Models (RLMs), GPU kernel optimization (KernelBench), and AI system architecture. Active in GPU Mode community.

## Key Topics

### GPU Kernels and AI-Generated Code
- AI-generated GPU kernels still leave room for human expertise
- Expert insights can replace massive token search efforts
- Most recent leaderboard solutions are AI-generated, but human verification remains critical

### Research Philosophy
- Successful PhD research comes from tackling problems "that most people in industry are not looking at"
- Graduate students should pursue ambitious, seemingly trivial ideas that industry labs won't risk resources on
- "If you don't have [the freedom to take big bets] as a PhD student and work on things that nobody cares about, the research is never gonna be that interesting"

### Recursive Language Models (RLMs)
- RLMs: harness design where code serves as primary tool
- Programmatic subagent calling and externalized context management
- Improves compositional generalization across diverse tasks
- Language models viewed as more than strictly autoregressive decoders

### Prime Agent
- Collaborative implementation building on RLM principles
- Introduces persistent subagents, agent-to-agent communication
- Continual harness design allows models to modify their own capabilities

### Agent Swarms and Scale
- OpenAI's Navier-Stokes solution: 10,000 agents, 130B output tokens over 88 hours, ~$40M compute
- Zhang questions efficiency of large-scale swarms vs. targeted approaches
- "The user doesn't wanna see [the underlying swarm] though, obviously, right? Like, it's not legible information"

### Open-Endedness in Research
- Research without fixed objectives, analogous to basic science
- Challenge: identifying valuable discoveries within massive generated datasets

### Sakana AI
- Praised for research-focused culture distinct from frontier model labs
- Commitment to exploratory work
