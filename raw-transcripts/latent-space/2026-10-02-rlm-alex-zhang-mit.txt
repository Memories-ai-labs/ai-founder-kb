## Episode Summary

Guest: Alex Zhang, MIT PhD student
Topics: Recursive Language Models (RLMs), GPU kernel optimization, AI agent systems, research strategy in academia

## Key Discussion Points

**GPU Programming & KernelBench**
Zhang discusses his involvement with GPU Mode, a community focused on kernel optimization. He highlights how AI-generated kernels still require human expertise for verification: "There definitely is [a gap from verifying]. I think, like, even with the most recent, like, math proofs and stuff, like, it doesn't necessarily mean mathematicians are obsolete."

**Research Taste & Academic Strategy**
Zhang emphasizes that successful PhD work involves taking unconventional bets: "If you don't have [big bets], I think the research is just never gonna be that interesting because you kind of need to take big bets if you're gonna be in academia." Theme: "Academia is for people taking risks on weird ideas that industry won't fund."

**Recursive Language Models (RLMs)**
RLMs represent a harness design enabling models to treat their own prompts as manipulable objects. Key features include:
- Context offloading
- Code-based tool execution
- Recursive subagent calling

**Harnesses as Compositional Generalizers**
Well-designed harnesses improve model generalization across domains by enforcing locally in-distribution task decomposition, enabling transfer between seemingly unrelated problems.

**Alternative Model Architectures**
Discussion includes Jev (classification-focused), loop transformers, and interaction models as alternatives to standard autoregressive decoders.

**Agent Swarms & OpenAI's Approach**
OpenAI's Navier-Stokes solution used 10,000 agents, 130 billion output tokens, and approximately $40 million in compute — illustrating both capability and inefficiency in current swarm architectures.

**Open-Endedness Research**
Zhang reflects on work at Sakana AI exploring open-ended problem generation without predefined objectives, acknowledging challenges in identifying valuable results from high-volume output.

**Key Thesis**
Future "language models" may be invisible agent scaffolds delivering simple user interfaces masking complex internal systems. The visible model is increasingly just the scaffolding layer.
