## Overview

Joon Sung Park discusses Simile AI's $2B Series B funding and the evolution from his viral "Generative Agents" research to creating digital twins that simulate human behavior with 85% accuracy. The conversation explores how simulation differs from prediction and its potential applications for major societal challenges.

## Key Topics Covered

**Background & Journey**
Park describes growing up in Korea before moving to Boston at age 11, initially pursuing painting before transitioning to computer science. He emphasizes how "the greatest artist often creates their own medium" and found that medium in computation.

**From Smallville to Simile**
The 2023 "Generative Agents" paper (Smallville) demonstrated that AI characters could develop emergent behaviors. This inspired the "time machine game" question: "What if we can just recreate the world that we live in?" Simulation emerged as more foundational than personal agents.

**Behavioral Foundation Models**
Rather than relying solely on web data, Simile uses three data categories:
- Interview data capturing life narratives
- Observational data (transactions, behavioral records)
- Randomized controlled trials revealing causal mechanisms

Park notes that "frontier models...do not consider the true attitudinal and behavioral aspect of people." His models aim to replicate human imperfection, not rationality.

**Evaluation & Accuracy**
The "Generative Agent Simulations of 1000 People" paper validated digital twins against real human behavior across personality tests, economic games, and behavioral experiments, achieving "85 percent as accurately as people would replicate their own" responses.

**Simulation vs. Prediction**
The critical distinction: simulation reveals *paths* to outcomes, not just predictions. The Foundation series' psychohistory analogy illustrates how counterintuitive steps may lead to desired futures — essential for decision-making.

**Scaling & Economics**
Current operations model tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of people. Future ambitions target simulating all 8 billion people, potentially requiring "an entire data center worth of simulations." Park believes this could become as costly as training foundation models but solve problems like climate change.

**Real-World Applications**
Customers include CVS, Gallup, Deloitte, and Wealthfront. Uses range from concept testing and focus groups to modeling earnings calls and UI testing. The technology replaces traditional human panels with "synthetic populations."

**Broader Vision**
Simulation addresses "wicked problems" including climate change, democratic instability, and policy questions like universal basic income. Park draws inspiration from Thomas Schelling's agent-based segregation models and sees simulation as a twin technology alongside AGI in advanced civilizations.

**Philosophical Framework**
Park compares simulation to painting — both highlight essential truths about subjects rather than achieving perfect representation. The work focuses on understanding "the mundane aspect of human lives" which collectively reveals societal truths.
