## Dyna-2 world-action model

- Trained on 1M+ hours of human video; predicts future video to guide robot actions (analogous to chain-of-thought, but in visual/physical space).
- Architecture: diffusion transformer using flow matching — faster action generation, easier to tune. Action space: ~100 continuous dimensions, predicted at high frequency.

## Data strategy

- Combines large-scale egocentric human video (broad physical priors) with smaller robot-specific data (low-level dexterity). Task-specific custom data also matters.
- Synthetic data via frontier models and Dyna's own world model could create self-reinforcing training loops.

## Deployment

- Latency requirements push toward on-device inference and model distillation. Dyna builds its own inference stack and some hardware, including actuators and a wheeled "semi-humanoid" robot (torso with 4 DOF).
- Currently folding thousands of napkins daily at Din Tai Fung restaurants; also working on laundry folding with Best Western.
- Goal for next year: deploy hundreds of robots.

## Architecture — agentic harnesses

- Hierarchical system wraps the model so robots can handle multi-step workflows lasting hours, not just short tasks.

## Bottlenecks

- Dexterous hands that overheat, limited sensing (touch, sound, infrared), and difficulty collecting data for complex tasks.

## Research perspective

- Ma argues world-action models (predicting raw future sensory states) may be more principled than VLA models (predicting abstract representations). Vision-only world models may miss important information.

## Background

- Path: interest in AlphaGo → PhD at Penn (RL for robotics) → Eureka project at NVIDIA (LLM-generated reward functions for simulated tasks) → Google DeepMind → co-founded Dyna late 2024.
- Name nods to Richard Sutton's Dyna architecture and a Japanese cartoon.
- Team: ~30–40% hardware, ~10 people on models. Research-plus-product culture.
