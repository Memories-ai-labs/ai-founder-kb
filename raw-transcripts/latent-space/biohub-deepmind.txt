## Key segments and themes

**Bitter lesson for data (00:00–04:50)**
Sal Candido: scaling only works once you find a scaling law, which depends on having data with the right information. Metagenomic data, though messy, improves protein language models. Both guests: choosing data based on availability can steer research away from the most important problems.

**Problem first (04:54–09:52)**
Pushmeet Kohli: researchers shouldn't be locked into being "a modeler" or "a data person." The problem should decide whether you need better models, more compute, or new data. AlphaFold relied on existing PDB data because building a larger dataset was out of reach. Cell genomics showed the data for a virtual cell isn't ready yet.

**Craft vs. scale (09:52–13:39)**
Kohli: AlphaFold's biophysics-informed design made it more data-efficient. Curating good data, with coverage and no redundancy, matters more than volume. Candido: the right inductive bias depends on data scale, and architecture work continues at scale. Not in a "post-Transformer world."

**Protein folding isn't solved (14:36–17:39)**
Kohli: AlphaFold predicted structures that were deposited in the PDB, not the full range of protein dynamics or disorder. Suggests cryo-EM micrographs could hold richer information. Candido: current models are like studying a bicycle spoke; the goal is to model whole systems.

**Design vs. understanding (20:43–23:48)**
Candido: models contain more knowledge than we've extracted, including information about function and motion. Kohli: calibrated confidence (like AlphaFold's pLDDT) is essential — a model that's confidently wrong isn't useful.

**Interpretability (23:48–27:04)**
Kohli separates behavioral understanding (knowing a model's strengths and limits) from mechanistic understanding. "Interpretable by whom" matters — future frontier models might interpret systems like AlphaFold better than humans can.

**Timelines (27:43–31:46)**
Kohli: AI is already used across drug discovery, but large speedups depend on solving harder biology. Candido: aiming for 10x improvements, not 10%, can reveal new approaches — tied to Biohub's mission to cure all disease.
