## Overview

The Latent Space team created a comprehensive tracker measuring how frontier AI models make recommendations across 161 product categories. They evaluated "6 prompt variations over 7 models" to identify patterns in agent-endorsed offerings (AEO).

## Key Findings

**Model Bias Patterns:**
Researchers documented clear self-preference behaviors: "Fable/Opus like Claude Code and Sol/Astra like Codex and Grok loves Cursor." However, they noted exceptions where GPT models recommended competing Claude products.

**Universal Preferences:**
Twenty-eight categories showed universal agreement among all tested models on a dominant choice, suggesting strong market leadership in those segments.

**Efficiency vs. Confidence:**
A notable difference emerged between model generations. The newer Astra model demonstrated greater confidence with fewer source consultations (median of 5 vs. Sol's 9), while Anthropic's Fable increased source-checking to 15 sources from Opus's 11.

**Technical Validation:**
The analysis confirmed that markdown content-negotiation practices — previously documented by other researchers — genuinely influence model reading behavior and recommendations.

## Methodology

- 7 frontier models tested
- 6 prompt variations per category
- 161 product categories evaluated
- All prompt-answer pairs publicly inspectable
- Interactive comparison tools and gamified exploration included

## Significance for AI Founders

- Model self-preference is real and measurable — product positioning for AI-native B2B must account for which model your target buyers use
- 28 categories show unanimous model consensus, signaling established market leaders that will be hard to dislodge via AEO alone
- Markdown formatting and content-negotiation genuinely affects model citations and recommendations — structured content wins
- Astra's higher confidence with fewer sources may signal a shift toward overconfident AI recommendations as models become more capable
