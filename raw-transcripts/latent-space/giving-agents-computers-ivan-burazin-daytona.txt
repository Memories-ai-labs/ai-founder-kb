## Summary / Key Discussion Points

Daytona provides composable computers and sandboxes optimized for AI agents rather than traditional code execution boxes. The platform emphasizes stateful, long-running environments with bare metal infrastructure and custom scheduling.

**Growth Metrics:** 74% month-on-month growth with 850,000 daily sandbox runs at peak. Performance benchmarks show ~60ms startup time for individual sandboxes and approximately 75 seconds to spin up 50,000 concurrently.

**Technical Architecture:** Rather than relying on virtual machines like competitors, Daytona uses bare metal servers with proprietary scheduling. This enables faster instantiation and dynamic resource resizing that prevents out-of-memory failures — capabilities "impossible on almost any other thing."

**Market Evolution:** Company initially served human developers but pivoted to AI agent infrastructure after recognizing distinct requirements. Reinforcement learning and evaluation workloads have grown from zero to roughly 50% of usage within months, creating unprecedented "spiky" demand patterns with zero-to-100,000 CPU spikes.

**Product Differentiators:**
- Computer use support (Windows, macOS, Linux sandboxes)
- Docker containerization with Sysbox hardening
- Open-source core (AGPL 3 licensed)
- Dramatic performance advantages versus Kubernetes alternatives (EKS/GKE)

**Business Model:** Targets B2B2C applications where agent-building companies consume infrastructure at scale, rather than direct developer sales. Resembles consumption-based platforms like Stripe or Twilio.

## Notable Quotes

- "The end of localhost" — Burazin's decade-long thesis

- On SaaS companies reselling tokens: "there will be cold shower when people understand" token resale alone doesn't constitute real acceleration.
