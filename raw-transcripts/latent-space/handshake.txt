## Episode Summary

Latent Space's first deep dive with an AI training data company. Handshake's AI training business reportedly grew from launch to ~$1 billion in gross annualized revenue in roughly a year. The episode asks what frontier labs buy at that scale and which evaluations models still fall well short of human experts on.

**Key topics:**

**How frontier labs buy training data:**
- How a lab evaluates a new dataset
- Testing data on open models
- Where rubrics fall short of expert judgment
- Why labs keep some evaluation tasks held out

**Finance benchmark (ATLAS-Finance):**
- Agents work through a simulated bank's inboxes, documents, and financial models
- Demo: an agent misapplying a tax treatment for existing goodwill — it can explain the rule when asked directly but fails to apply it in the relevant step of its spreadsheet work
- Excel grader scoring: penalty traps, dependency gates, partial credit

**Biology benchmark (VIALS):**
- Benchmark for interpreting scientific images: gel band reading, identifying necrosis in brain-tissue slides
- Whether Python tools help models read images
- How vision-language models were trained on scientific figures

**Alignment and coding benchmarks:**
- Simulated file-access tasks tracking which files an agent read, whether it checked copies, and how it changed shell history
- CAREBench safety evaluation
- How safety feedback reaches training
- Fair-lending loan-application test

**Business:**
- Private benchmarks for enterprises
- When enterprises need their own models
- Market for compute and expert data
- Hiring at Handshake AI

## Signal for AI Founders

Handshake AI reaching $1B ARR in ~1 year from launching a training data business illustrates the massive and urgent demand from frontier labs for expert human evaluation and specialized datasets. The company is essentially monetizing the gap between model capabilities and human expert performance.
