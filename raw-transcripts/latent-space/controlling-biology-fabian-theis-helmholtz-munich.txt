## Episode Summary

The episode covers how systems biology is moving from mapping biology toward predicting and controlling it. Theis has been a central figure in Virtual Cell efforts.

**Key topics:**

**Why now — funding context:**
- Biohub's $1.8B in commitments from Biohub, NIH, DOE, Google, and Meta signals major institutional bet on computational biology.
- Timing driven by convergence of better single-cell data, foundation models, and compute.

**Perturbation prediction:**
- Simple baselines often outperform complex models — partly because evaluation metrics are poorly calibrated.
- The harder problem: modeling multi-scale, co-registered biological data across molecules, cells, tissues, and organisms.

**Virtual Cells:**
- The concept of Virtual Cells (digital twins of biological systems) is becoming more concrete.
- Theis argues for multi-scale, co-registered data as the key ingredient beyond model architecture.

**The Bitter Lesson in Biology:**
- More data and compute tends to win over hand-designed biological priors.
- Scale-based approach is now dominant in computational biology as in NLP.

**Lab-in-the-loop:**
- Closing the loop between model predictions and wet-lab validation is the next frontier.
- Theis's group at Helmholtz is working on automated lab integration.

**Precision medicine:**
- Helmholtz work on individual-level drug response prediction.
- Foundation models trained on large patient cohorts enable personalized treatment suggestions.

**Business of Virtual Cells:**
- Multiple startups emerging in this space.
- Key question: who owns the data, and can you build a moat on biological foundation models?

## Significance for AI Founders

Virtual Cell technology is approaching commercialization readiness. The convergence of biological foundation models, automated labs, and large-scale data creates a window for companies building at this intersection. The "bitter lesson" framing — scale wins over prior-based models — mirrors what happened in NLP and signals where to invest R&D attention.
