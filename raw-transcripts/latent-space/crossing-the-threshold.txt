# Crossing the Threshold: How OpenAI and Anthropic Approach Autonomy and Safety in Financial Agents

# Date: 2026-10-14
# Source: Latent Space

Author: Richard MacManus
Published: October 14, 2026

## Summary

At the AI Engineer New York conference, frontier AI labs and financial firms discussed how to give agents useful autonomy while keeping consequential decisions under control. The main takeaway: safe autonomy depends on where controls and human judgment are placed, not on how many restrictions are imposed.

## Key Speakers and Points

**OpenAI — Joseph Kim, Head of ChatGPT for Financial Services**
- Described a "threshold of usefulness" — the point where a model is reliable enough to save users time
- Predicted that by 2027, agents would be capable of investment idea generation and trading
- Stressed traceability: finance professionals need to verify where numbers come from
- Framed human-in-the-loop design as a product question about what users should control, review, or approve

**Two Sigma — John Feminella, SVP Architecture**
- Warned against "approval fatigue" — where people approving every decision become less effective and introduce risk
- Two Sigma uses composable controls, including auto-mode classifiers that route only the most important cases to humans
- Classifiers handle ambiguous cases; deterministic rules handle clear ones
- Argued controls should enable autonomy rather than create roadblocks — compared to how brakes let a car "go fast and stop safely"

**Anthropic — Belinda Neal and Keon Shahab**
- Discussed building a "trust layer" for agents, tied to Claude for Financial Advisors
- Layer covers: identity and permissions, sandboxing with approval steps before actions, confirming agents remain reliable as conditions change
- Example: a trade break agent that proposes order amendments for human approval rather than making them directly

**Bloomberg — Andrey Rybka**
- Outlined defenses: scoped credentials, tool-call controls, outbound traffic restrictions, audit trails, budgets, and kill switches
- Recommended input validation, prompt injection defenses, and human approval for high-value actions
- Emphasized keeping controls outside the model

## Key Themes

1. The threshold of usefulness — agents need to be reliable enough to save time before deployment
2. Approval fatigue is a real risk — too many human approvals reduce safety by reducing attention
3. Trust layers: identity, sandboxing, reliability monitoring are the key components
4. Controls outside the model are more robust than controls inside the model
5. Finance is the first major regulated vertical to operationalize agentic AI at scale
