## Overview
Formalization of independent AI evaluation standards, featuring significant endorsements from xAI, OpenAI, and Anthropic. Covers escalating debates over whether frontier AI companies should prioritize capability pacing or focus on containment and safety controls.

## Key Developments

### AEF-1 Standard Launch
The AI Evaluator Forum published AEF-1, establishing baseline criteria for third-party evaluations addressing access, conflicts of interest, funding transparency, and recusal protocols.

### Anthropic's Embedded Evaluator Commitment
Dario Amodei announced unilateral commitments including permanent evaluator access to offices, badges, and company laptops, positioning this as precedent-setting transparency comparable to banking industry regulatory oversight.

### The Pacing Debate
Sharp public division between advocates for slowing capability development and those favoring control-focused mitigation strategies. Concerns about "situationally aware models" potentially gaming evaluations versus arguments emphasizing engineering-based governance and production safeguards.

### Opposition Perspectives
Critics raised concerns about Silicon Valley companies serving as AI gatekeepers for governments; questions about whether AI risk rhetoric had become speculative rather than grounded in immediate engineering problems.

## Key Theme
Governance increasingly resembles production engineering—managing systems through tool routing, monitoring, kill switches, and oversight rather than relying solely on model-level safety measures.
