## Summary / Show Notes

Anjney Midha, CEO of AMP, joins Swyx to discuss compute infrastructure, "outputmaxxing" as a philosophy, and lessons from his time at Anthropic and as a VC.

---

## Key Themes

### Compute Utilization & Efficiency

Midha emphasizes that frontier AI infrastructure suffers from significant waste. At Google, 95% node utilization "was considered an outage," while best-in-class Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU) today reaches 60-70%. Many frontier labs underperform because funding and operations teams lack alignment across the supply chain.

### AMP's Vision: Making "FLOPs Flow Like Megawatts"

AMP positions itself as an independent system operator for compute — similar to electricity grid operators. Rather than vertical integration, the approach pools compute across multiple clouds and silicon architectures. This horizontal model aims to create fungible compute resources through scheduling, economics, and emerging protocols.

### Infrastructure & Community Relations

Midha advocates for "responsible infrastructure" that benefits local communities. He suggests sharing marginal compute cost increases with communities hosting data centers, noting that "up to 20% of data centers this year in the US are at risk" of community backlash around power grids and permitting.

### End-of-Life Prediction in Healthcare

A 14-year passion project: using AI for precise patient mortality prediction — empowering informed decisions while reducing Medicare waste. Over 30% of Medicare/Medicaid spending goes to end-of-life care, often deteriorating quality of life unnecessarily.

### "Output Maxing" as Discipline

The core philosophy: achieving optimal outcomes through efficiency rather than just scaling inputs. This applies to GPU utilization, architectural choices, and human resources — avoiding wastage at all scales.

### Culture as Fragile Moat

Midha argues culture isn't a durable competitive advantage but rather "a very brittle, fragile thing that requires daily tending." Anthropic exemplified this through hardship-driven alignment — scarcity forced clarity on priorities (coding as P0), building missionary conviction around safety.

### Researcher-CEOs & Leadership

The discussion challenges venture capital's tendency to box researchers as non-founders. Great scientists demonstrate leadership through publication at top venues, making them "star athletes of the mind." Being a strong CEO requires willingness to be "confrontational up and down the stack."

### Silicon Valley's Dual Nature

Midha reflects on Silicon Valley as both missionary (mission-driven) and mercenary (money-motivated). Early hardship — his upbringing in Rishi Valley and cramped Singapore dormitories — shaped his view that money should fund missions, not serve as a primary measure of success.
