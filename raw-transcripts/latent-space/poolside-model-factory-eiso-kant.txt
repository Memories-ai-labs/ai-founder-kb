## Overview

Eiso Kant discusses how Poolside built a "model factory" capable of training Laguna S, a 118B parameter mixture-of-experts model that outperforms significantly larger competitors. The episode covers the company's journey from early work on code models to creating systems that enable rapid model development cycles.

## Key Topics Discussed

**Company Origins & Philosophy**
Kant was inspired by Andrej Karpathy's 2015 work on recurrent neural networks to build language models for code. After spending four years and "$12 million" on the earlier Sourced venture before ChatGPT vindicated the approach, he co-founded Poolside with a commitment to open-source AI development.

**The Model Factory**
Poolside's engineering infrastructure enables fewer than 70 researchers to run "10,000–20,000 experiments each month." Key innovations include streaming training data directly (avoiding batch preprocessing delays) and maintaining immutable data layers for reproducibility.

**Laguna S Model**
The 118B parameter model with 8B active tokens was developed in eight weeks. Kant emphasizes that gains come from "persistence, verification, and backtracking" rather than raw parameter count alone.

**Research Philosophy**
The company openly shares technical reports and ablation studies, believing this accelerates the broader field. Kant advocates for "100 foundation model companies" rather than industry consolidation.

**Model vs. Harness**
Poolside maintains a minimal harness approach, preferring models that write code directly rather than selecting from predefined tools. Kant calls traditional tool-call approaches "stupid," arguing models should have freedom to solve problems through custom scripts.

**Future Direction**
Planned work includes vision capabilities and exploring how reinforcement learning moves earlier into pretraining. Kant questions whether peak performance for knowledge work requires massive models, suggesting smaller efficient models may handle many tasks adequately.
