SUMMARY: AI engineers are rediscovering ontologies to constrain probabilistic language models. "Data as graphs" serve as "logical guardrails" for agentic systems. This represents a 2026 shift toward software engineering discipline after 2025's "vibe coding" trend.

---

CONTRIBUTORS AND KEY POINTS:

Frank Coyle (UC Berkeley):
- Advocates "neurosymbolic AI" combining neural networks with rule-based systems
- Ontologies provide structural constraints that LLMs lack

Emil Eifrem (Neo4j):
- Proposes three ontology types for enterprise agents:
  1. Business-facing ontologies
  2. Technical ontologies
  3. Execution traces
- Knowledge graphs as the structured backbone for agent memory

Kingsley Idehen (OpenLink Software):
- Developing "agent engineering stack" using RDF memory
- Established frameworks like Schema.org, FOAF, and Dublin Core "define the types of entities and relationships through which language acquires computable context"

---

CENTRAL PROBLEM:
LLMs excel at probabilistic reasoning but lack structural constraints. Ontologies provide guardrails that prevent hallucination and ensure consistent entity/relationship handling in multi-step agentic workflows.

CHALLENGE:
Maintenance and upkeep of ontologies remain difficult. Some propose that agents could self-maintain ontologies during operation — a promising but unproven approach.

BROADER CONTEXT:
This piece represents a broader 2026 industry shift: after 2025's "vibe coding" era, there is growing demand for quality control, formal structures, and human oversight in agent loops. Ontologies — once considered outdated Semantic Web artifacts — are finding new relevance as the engineering discipline of AI matures.
