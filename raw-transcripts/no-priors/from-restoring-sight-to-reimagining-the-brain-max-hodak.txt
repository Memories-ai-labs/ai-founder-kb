## Overview

Max Hodak, co-founder and CEO of Science.xyz (formerly Neuralink co-founder), discusses Science's breakthrough retinal implant called PRIMA, which recently gained European regulatory approval. The conversation explores vision restoration, brain-computer interfaces, and philosophical questions about consciousness and human identity.

## Key Topics

### Vision Restoration Technology

Science developed PRIMA, a retinal prosthesis comparable to cochlear implants for the eye. The device works by implanting a chip beneath the retina that converts visual signals from specialized glasses into neural stimulation, bypassing damaged photoreceptor cells.

### Clinical Success

According to Hodak: "Nobody had previously ever been able to restore a form vision image in the mind's eye of a blind patient in this way." Trial participants demonstrated remarkable results, including reading books and solving puzzles.

### Broader Vision

Hodak articulates that "the brain very literally, very clearly, plainly is a computer," emphasizing computational approaches to medical challenges rather than traditional pharmaceutical development.

### Future Roadmap

Science aims for three primary initiatives:
1. Vision restoration (PRIMA retinal implant)
2. Bio-hybrid neural interfaces
3. A perfusion medicine program called Vessel
