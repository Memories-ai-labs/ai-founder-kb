## Show Notes Summary

The episode explores why standard AI benchmarking practices have become inadequate. Noam Brown argues that traditional benchmark grids fail to account for test-time compute—the computational resources allocated during inference. As models like o1 demonstrate, capability scaling depends heavily on thinking budget rather than being fixed.

## Key Points

**Benchmark Methodology:**
Current benchmark presentations don't control for inference compute, making model comparisons misleading. Brown states: "the capability of the model is a function of how much money you put into it, basically."

**Latent Capabilities:**
Previously released models contain significant untapped potential that users haven't fully explored. Models like o1 can improve for weeks on certain tasks before plateauing, making static benchmarks inadequate measures of capability.

**Safety Implications:**
Existing safety frameworks don't address how dangerous capabilities scale with increased compute budgets. Current safety frameworks don't specify evaluation budgets, creating ambiguity about what capability level labs are actually assessing for dangerous tasks.

**Research Timeline Tensions:**
Model release cycles conflict with evaluation timelines that might require months or years of computation.

**Recursive Self-Improvement Limits:**
Large inference times create natural bottlenecks that prevent overnight intelligence explosions, as models require extended computation periods for maximum capability.

**Research Acceleration:**
While models accelerate researcher productivity significantly, they lack the "research taste" needed for novel algorithmic breakthroughs without extensive scaffolding.

**Future Directions:**
Multi-agent coordination at scale remains largely unexplored, with potential for models to accumulate and share knowledge globally.

**Practical Applications:**
Brown shares examples from building poker-solving bots and the Erdős unit distance conjecture work. Poker bot development used as a personal evaluation metric, noting progression from early model failures to near-complete solver capabilities with recent models.

## Central Recommendation

Publish benchmark results with test-time compute (tokens, cost, or time) as the x-axis rather than presenting static performance grids.
