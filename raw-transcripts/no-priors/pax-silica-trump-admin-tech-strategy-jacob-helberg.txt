## Overview

Helberg describes Pax Silica as "an economic security coalition that now has 14 countries" focused on securing the AI supply chain through an ecosystems-based approach. The initiative announced a forward-deployed industrial base in the Philippines, with 4,000 acres designated as an "economic security zone."

## Key Themes

### Contrasts with China's Belt and Road
Unlike China's state-operated model, Helberg emphasizes: "We're not going to do government-operated supply chains because that's not how we shine as a country." The U.S. approach leverages private sector expertise, positioning companies rather than government entities as drivers.

### Supply Chain Challenges
Covers concentration risks across AI inputs including "precision reducers and server motors and rare earth magnets and actuators," with particular focus on robotics supply chain dominance by China.

### America as Underdog
Helberg characterizes America as possessing inherent underdog qualities: founders embody "contrarian idea[s]" challenging established orthodoxies, reflecting a cultural DNA enabling national resilience during setbacks.

### Pax Silica Framework
- 14-country economic security coalition
- Forward-deployed industrial base in Philippines (4,000 acres)
- Ecosystems-based approach to AI supply chain security
- Private sector as primary driver (vs. state-operated)
