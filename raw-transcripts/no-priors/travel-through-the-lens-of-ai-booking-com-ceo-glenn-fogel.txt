## Episode Overview

Glenn Fogel discusses how he navigated Booking.com's transformation from joining Priceline in 2000 (near the NASDAQ peak), witnessed the stock fall to $1/share, and presided over a 1000x recovery. Now leading the AI transformation of travel, he discusses why AI won't eliminate travel intermediaries but will transform them.

---

## Key Discussion Points

### Career Journey and Resilience
- Joined Priceline in 2000 via Wharton/Harvard Law and Wall Street banking
- Stayed through the dot-com crash when stock fell to $1/share
- The experience shaped his view on hype cycles and long-term value

### Lessons from Internet Era Applied to AI
"There always been these kind of speculative booms that create tremendous innovation."
- Disappointment will occur, but significant companies will emerge
- Cites historical examples: California Gold Rush (the pickaxe sellers won)
- Key insight: the infrastructure plays often outlast the speculative ones

### Travel + AI Strategy
- Travel planning is genuinely complex — multi-destination, family considerations, preferences
- AI enables better customer experiences rather than eliminating travel companies
- Priceline's "Penny" AI agent helps users navigate complex trip planning
- Positioned AI as a tool for personalization at scale, not disintermediation

### Scale as Durable Asset
- 8.6 million alternative accommodation listings
- $186 billion in annual travel processed
- "There is no such thing as a moat" — continuous innovation required
- Scale provides data advantages that compound with AI training

### Investment Philosophy
- ~$700 million annually invested in technology and AI
- $4 billion in Q1 buybacks and dividends — returning capital while investing
- Believes internal AI investment beats vendor dependency for core capabilities

### AI and Employment
- Company's own experience with machine translation eliminating translation jobs
- Emphasis on upskilling current employees rather than relying on government retraining
- Believes companies have responsibility to reskill workers for AI-adjacent roles
