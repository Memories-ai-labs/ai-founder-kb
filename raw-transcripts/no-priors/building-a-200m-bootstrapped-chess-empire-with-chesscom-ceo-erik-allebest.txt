## Episode Summary

Erik Allebest, CEO of Chess.com, discusses building a $200M+ bootstrapped company with 250M registered members and 10M daily active users. Key founder-relevant topics:

**Bootstrapping to scale**: Chess.com was acquired out of bankruptcy in 2005 for $56,000. VCs rejected it as uninvestable. Allebest bootstrapped for years before accepting PE investment from General Atlantic and CVC Capital Partners.

**AI and human competition**: Despite computers surpassing humans at chess 30 years ago, interest has grown — "humans want to do human stuff." AI enhances rather than replaces the game, used for training and analysis. A key insight for founders building in AI-adjacent markets: AI capability doesn't kill user interest in human performance.

**Cheating detection at scale**: Chess.com invests heavily in ML-based anti-cheating, detecting when players use computer assistance. Applies at both casual and professional levels — lesson in building trust systems at scale.

**Cultural shifts and timing**: Chess went from "nerd" to "cool" post-Queen's Gambit + social media. Allebest's insight on riding cultural waves rather than forcing them.

**Scale metrics**: >$200M ARR, 250M registered members, 10M DAU, 40-50M MAU. Launched Gambit (poker) applying same rating/skill-measurement model.

**Key founder insight**: Building a platform around a timeless activity (skill-based competition) creates durable defensibility. The rating system is the moat — not the feature set.
