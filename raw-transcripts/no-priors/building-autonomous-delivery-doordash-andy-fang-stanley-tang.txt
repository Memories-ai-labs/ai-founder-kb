## Ask DoorDash — Agentic Commerce

Natural language interface enabling conversational ordering for food and groceries.
- 50% of restaurant orders through Ask DoorDash come from new establishments for users
- Grocery basket sizes increased approximately 40% with the new interface
- Natural language reduces friction vs traditional search and keyword optimization
- Integration of real-world knowledge helps users discover trending restaurants

Notable quote: "We are seeing people 50% of trajectories of people using Ask DoorDash for restaurants. 50% of those trajectories are people ordering from places they've never ordered from before."

## Robotics Investment Timeline

DoorDash began exploring autonomous delivery robotics in 2018, treating it initially as a skunk works project rather than an immediate production initiative.

## Dot — The Autonomous Delivery Robot

Specifications:
- Weighs 300 pounds
- Travels up to 20 miles per hour
- Designed for sidewalks, bike lanes, and roads
- Fully autonomous Level 4 capability
- Operating in Phoenix for over two years

Design philosophy quote: "If you're trying to solve that three to five-mile delivery in the suburb, which is where most of the deliveries happen, the right metaphor is probably an autonomous motorcycle or a scooter or a bike profile vehicle."

## The "First and Last 100 Feet Problem"

Stanley Tang explained the critical challenge of pickup and delivery precision:
- Robots must locate exact merchant pickup points
- Delivery requires finding precise residential drop-off locations
- Human dashers can intuitively navigate these situations; robots cannot
- DoorDash's historical data of actual delivery locations provides unique competitive advantage

## Operational Challenges at Scale

Real-world deployment complexities:
- Camera sensor obstruction from dirt and debris
- Variable surface conditions (leaves on roads affecting wheel torque)
- Battery management during emergency braking scenarios
- Boot-up processes becoming productivity bottlenecks with fleet growth
- Merchant integration variations across geographies

Quote: "Things start breaking. Like how do you handle operations? Like people don't think about actually in order to scale autonomy. There's a lot of non-autonomy or like operations."

## Multimodal Strategy

DoorDash employs matched solutions rather than one-size-fits-all:
- Suburban 3-5 mile deliveries: Dot robots
- Rural areas with poor infrastructure: Lightweight robots or drones
- Complex multi-step orders: Human dashers

Philosophy: "You don't have, it's not an all or nothing approach. You can kind of phase in these modalities over time."

## Data Advantage

DoorDash's competitive positioning:
- 10 billion historical delivery data points
- 40+ million monthly consumers
- Understanding of merchant integration patterns across 50+ countries
- Real-world pickup and drop-off location data unavailable to competitors

## AI and Internal Productivity

Andy Fang on internal AI adoption:
- AI spending increased 20x from January to June 2024
- Recently achieved flat spending through optimization benchmarking
- Released DashBench to measure model performance on coding tasks
- Focus on ROI calculation for frontier model usage
- Expanding AI beyond engineering to analytics, operations, and merchant management

## Future Vision

On Human Dashers: Stanley Tang predicted growth rather than replacement: "In a world where robotics, drones, AI is everywhere. My guess is that in 10 years' time, we're actually going to have more dashers doing deliveries, not less."

On Agent-First Commerce: Andy Fang suggested emerging use cases like autonomous pantry restocking based on camera monitoring, enabled by CLI interfaces.

Scaling constraints: shifting from autonomy (now solved) to operations, commercialization, and hardware manufacturing.
