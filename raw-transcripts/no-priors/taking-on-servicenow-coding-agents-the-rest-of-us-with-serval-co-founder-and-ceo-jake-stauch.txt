Guest: Jake Stauch, Co-Founder and CEO of Serval

**Episode Summary**

Serval co-founder and CEO Jake Stauch discusses how his company is challenging ServiceNow with AI-driven enterprise automation.

**Origin:** Stauch saw IT teams frustrated by ServiceNow's high cost and slow customization, which often required consultants and months of implementation.

**Core thesis:** Code is a better way to express automation than drag-and-drop builders, and AI made it practical for non-engineers in IT and other departments to write it.

**Guardrails:** Serval works as a ticketing system with deterministic approvals, staged releases, audit trails, and just-in-time elevated access. Stauch describes Serval as "the boundary" and "the guardrails," rather than a developer of LLMs.

**Go-to-market:** Serval sells top-down to C-level and IT leadership. Its ProServal automation engineers embed with customers for ongoing support. Customer requests that recur across accounts can become platform features.

**Catalyst:** An agent that helps admins build automations and configure approval and permission policies.

**Outlook:** Stauch expects personal agents to enter the enterprise but require governance. He sees SaaS consolidation around point solutions rather than the disappearance of core systems of record. He calls background agents a moonshot, with autonomy expanding incrementally through approvals.

**ROI:** Customers want proof that AI spend pays off, so Serval prices based on workflows run rather than tokens.

**Team:** Grew from ~20 people in January 2026 to ~200, with product decisions pushed down to engineers who talk to customers constantly.
