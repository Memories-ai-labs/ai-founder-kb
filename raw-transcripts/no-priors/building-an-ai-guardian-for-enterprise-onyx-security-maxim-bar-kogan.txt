# Building an AI Guardian for Enterprise with Onyx Security CEO Maxim Bar Kogan
# Guest: Maxim Bar Kogan, CEO & Co-founder, Onyx Security; Sarah Guo (Host)
# URL: https://podscripts.co/podcasts/no-priors-artificial-intelligence-technology-startups/building-an-ai-guardian-for-enterprise-with-onyx-security-ceo-maxim-bar-kogan
# Date: 2026-05-28
# source: No Priors: Artificial Intelligence | Technology | Startups

[00:00:00] Sarah Guo opens discussing how autonomous AI agents increasingly manage critical infrastructure, raising security concerns about agent oversight and control at enterprise scale.

[00:00:47] Maxim Bar Kogan introduces Onyx Security, an Israel-based startup building systems to monitor AI agents. The discussion shifts to how market conditions changed since the company's founding around the AutoGPT release.

[00:02:12] Maxim explains AutoGPT as "the first really autonomous agent running on LLMs" that let systems decide actions and access APIs in loops, enabling complex autonomous capabilities.

[00:03:24] Discussion of early timing challenges — enterprises initially weren't using agents, and security budgets were skeptical. However, reasoning models and tools like Claude eventually validated the market need.

[00:05:30] Sarah asks about Onyx's current product. Maxim describes their work training oversight models and productizing via an "AI control plane" to monitor agent actions and prevent illegitimate operations.

[00:07:07] Maxim notes enterprises face exponential growth in agent actions, with incidents like agents publishing tokens or code unexpectedly. Existing security tools lack context to understand agent intent.

[00:08:38] On current enterprise deployment: approximately 50% autonomous coding agents, 45% low-code automations, and 2% first-party agents. Autonomous tools represent the fastest-growing category.

[00:10:47] Traditional security controls (identity, endpoint, API security) struggle with flexible AI agents that require broad permissions. Existing tools lack visibility into agent reasoning.

[00:13:17] Sarah questions proxy-based approaches. Maxim explains proxies face technical limitations across cloud/endpoint/local deployments and, critically, "the hard problem is understanding what I should do now."

[00:14:52] On training proprietary models: running a separate capable agent for oversight creates cost, latency, and reliability problems. Instead, Onyx trains small, efficient models that flag when escalation is needed.

[00:17:33] Chess analogy: like chess masters making intuitive moves with occasional deep calculations during critical moments, oversight models make rapid assessments but escalate to deeper analysis when risks are highest.

[00:18:50] Onyx's talent combines Israeli intelligence backgrounds in mathematics and cyber with AI expertise (co-founder Gil worked on synthetic data at Nvidia). The team reflects intersection of security and AI fields.

[00:20:39] Onyx positioned as independent oversight, not vendor-supplied by foundation model labs. Long-term vision involves mechanistic interpretability research to understand model internals.

[00:22:02] On mechanistic interpretability: Maxim believes understanding model weights and activations becomes feasible as models become more intelligent than humans, enabling better oversight of super-intelligent systems.

[00:23:39] Building customer trust despite being a young company: enterprises are motivated by acute pain from agent-related incidents and understand betting on early promising vendors reduces future risk.

[00:26:00] On vulnerability discovery acceleration: Maxim acknowledges the rapid pace of automated vulnerability research represents a fundamental shift requiring foundational security investments beyond quick fixes.

[00:27:34] Regarding phased rollout (Glasswing/Daybreak): Maxim suggests assuming mythical models arrive regardless and emphasizes enterprises need foundational controls now to mitigate downstream vulnerability exploitation.

[00:29:52] Most large enterprise bans on AI adoption have dissolved. Companies now adopt multiple tools but may implement different risk profiles based on sector and size, with even conservative firms like JP Morgan adopting AI.

[00:31:03] Onyx's scope remains focused on two stable AI primitives: LLM foundation models and agent loop structures. Strong opinions held loosely about 2026; more openness to paradigm shifts by 2027.

[00:33:24] On labs addressing trust/governance: Independent oversight vendors are preferred, similar to how car certification doesn't come from manufacturers. Vendors have incentives labs don't regarding accuracy and accountability.

[00:34:28] Two categories of agent failures: jagged intelligence (silly mistakes improving with better models) and emerging semi-conscious model perspectives misaligned with organizational values — the latter harder for vendors to address.

[00:36:01] Onyx gains advantages through accessing historical agent behavior data enterprises won't share with foundation model providers, plus managing multiple vendors rather than relying on single-source solutions.

[00:37:44] Israeli security expertise advantage: deep understanding of how security teams operate daily, their organizational structures, responsibilities, and what drives decision-making and praise within security functions.

[00:40:05] Maxim clarifies being "AGI-pilled" while focusing current products on human users: future agents will eventually run security functions, but today's experience design matters for adoption. The principle remains understanding end-users' needs.

[00:41:04] Episode concludes with standard podcast promotion across Twitter, YouTube, Apple Podcasts, and Spotify.
