# Who Gets to Define the Rules for AI?
# Author: Aidan Gomez (Co-Founder & CEO, Cohere)
# URL: https://cohere.com/blog/who-gets-to-define-the-rules-for-ai
# Date: 2026-09-13
# Source: Cohere Blog

Artificial intelligence is fundamentally reshaping society. The central question: "Should a handful of market-dominant Silicon Valley AI companies define safety standards and regulations for the entire world?"

## Historical Parallels

The essay draws parallels to past regulatory failures. In 1975, the SEC designated three bond-rating firms as gatekeepers, creating a protected market. These firms later rated subprime securities triple-A, nearly collapsing the global economy. The EU's 1985 Motor Vehicle Block Exemption allowed manufacturers to set safety standards, creating monopolistic protections that took 25 years to unwind.

## The Current Proposal

Recent proposals from major AI labs seek antitrust exemptions under the guise of safety coordination — having "a handful of powerful labs agree on shared standards" while requesting governmental antitrust waivers, then requiring all others to follow predetermined rules without input.

## Four Pillars for Better Governance

1. **Evidence-based risk framework:** Establish published accounts of specific harms, which capabilities cause them, and contextual conditions requiring intervention. Build collaboratively across nations, openly rather than behind national security claims.

2. **Mandatory transparency:** Developers should disclose how systems are built, intended purposes, capabilities, risks, and mitigation measures. Incident reporting mechanisms should ensure lessons benefit the entire field.

3. **Scoped testing:** Independent testing should target genuine dangers — cyberattacks, synthetic fraud, voice cloning, manipulation, weapons development, and critical infrastructure threats. Testing should scale proportionally with capability level and deployment context.

4. **Real assurance mechanisms:** Adopt layered approaches similar to financial, aviation, and nuclear industries. Testing standards must be collectively developed and published. Third-party evaluators require diverse mandates, never financial conflicts with reviewed parties.

## Key Quote

"More voices makes it slower and harder, and some will say unwelcome things — that's precisely the point." Only this approach produces rulesets the public has legitimate reason to trust.

## Cohere's Commitment

Build responsibly at sustainable speeds, enable partner sovereignty, and collaborate with lawmakers and safety experts rather than seeking regulatory capture.
