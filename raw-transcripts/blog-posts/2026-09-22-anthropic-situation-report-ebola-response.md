# The Situation Report: Claude Accelerating Ebola Outbreak Response
# URL: https://www.anthropic.com/features/ebola-response
# Date: 2026-09-22
# Source: Anthropic

**Date:** September 22, 2026

## Full Article Text

In Butembo, eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, community health workers document cases door-to-door, sending daily notes via WhatsApp to zone managers. These records form the foundation of situation reports that guide outbreak response decisions. Until recently, compiling these reports required teams to work late into the night.

A rare Bundibugyo ebolavirus strain, lacking an approved vaccine, spreads through eastern DRC. As of the report date, 7,672 confirmed cases and 3,699 deaths had been recorded across seven provinces, with a 48.2% fatality rate. The outbreak, confirmed in May, was declared an international concern on May 17.

Health workers gather critical data—symptom onset, contacts, and locations—in notebooks. Speed matters: finding infected individuals quickly, before critical illness develops, determines survival likelihood. The traditional data chain moved slowly: health facilities sent information via WhatsApp to districts, which created PowerPoint slides forwarded to coordination centers, then to provincial ministries where staff compiled reports working late nights.

WHO AFRO, CEPI, and INRB partnered with Anthropic to deploy Claude across three response areas:

**Situation Report Acceleration:** Tendai Muza and colleagues built a Claude skill extracting case numbers from PowerPoint decks, identifying trend changes, and summarizing district reports. Processing time dropped from all day to under one hour.

**Vaccine Development Coordination:** Claude organized complex, multi-factor data across proposals, enabling faster comparison and robust tracking of actions needed for advancing vaccine development.

**Genome Analysis:** Claude Science reduced bioinformatics bottlenecks in outbreak labs. Scientists can now prompt the system in plain language to assemble viral genomes and construct genetic family trees, accelerating variant identification and outbreak scale estimation.

Dr. Jean-Jacques Muyembe, DRC's National Institute of Biomedical Research Director General, emphasized: "You beat Ebola by knowing where it is today, not where it was last week."

WHO AFRO's Paul Ouma noted that previously time constraints allowed only single disease models. Now multiple models run simultaneously, enabling forecasts for treatment center placement decisions.

CEPI's Polina Brangel stated that Claude "organize[s] complex, multi-factor data in a way that allows the human eye to compare across a wide range of proposals" within shortened timeframes.

WHO Africa plans expanding these tools to other outbreaks, including chikungunya response, and intends stitching historical disease databases together for informed future response decisions.
