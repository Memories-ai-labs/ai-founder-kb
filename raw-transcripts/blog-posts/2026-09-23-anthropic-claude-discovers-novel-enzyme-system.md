# Claude Discovers a Novel Enzyme System with CRISPR-like Repeats
# URL: https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-discovers-novel-enzyme-system
# Date: 2026-09-23
# Source: Anthropic

**Date:** September 23, 2026

## Full Article Text

Anthropic announced the formation of a new life sciences research group and laboratory focused on fundamental biology research using Claude. The initiative explores DNA datasets to identify uncharacterized protein families, generate hypotheses at scale, and test them through laboratory experiments.

The team shared early results showing Claude's autonomous discovery of a novel enzyme system called array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART), which exhibits properties reminiscent of CRISPR technology. The system was found in bacteriophages and consists of a reverse transcriptase, a partner gene, and an array of evenly spaced DNA repeat sequences.

Claude agents searched through a massive database of DNA sequences over 21 hours, using approximately 950 agents and consuming 210 million tokens. One agent identified a repeating pattern of DNA sequences adjacent to an unusual reverse transcriptase gene—a discovery that sparked further analysis and laboratory testing.

The research team, specialists in computational biology with expertise in CRISPR systems and enzyme discovery, conducted biochemical and structural characterization of the ART system. They found that the ART array is expressed as distinct short RNAs, suggesting programmable functionality similar to CRISPR systems.

Feng Zhang, MIT professor and CRISPR pioneer, commented: "This is an exciting example of how AI agents can contribute to biological discovery" and noted the findings merit further investigation.

The researchers emphasized this discovery demonstrates AI's capability for autonomous anomaly detection and hypothesis generation in biological research. They released a detailed technical pre-print and expressed interest in collaborating with other scientists on similar research questions.
