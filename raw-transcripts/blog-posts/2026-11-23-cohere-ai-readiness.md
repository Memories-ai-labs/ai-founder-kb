## Overview

This post argues that AI readiness covers much more than choosing the right technology. It presents a seven-dimension framework for assessing an organization's AI readiness.

## The Seven Dimensions

1. **Strategy and leadership alignment** — Do executives have a clear AI vision and are they aligned on investment and risk tolerance?
2. **AI technical expertise** — Does the team have the skills to build, deploy, and maintain AI systems?
3. **Technology and infrastructure** — Is the existing tech stack ready to integrate AI, with appropriate compute and APIs?
4. **Governance, risk, and compliance** — Are there policies and processes for responsible AI use?
5. **Data readiness** — Is data accessible, clean, and properly governed?
6. **Organizational change** — Is the company culturally ready to adopt AI and manage workflow changes?
7. **Use case discovery and prioritization** — Has the organization identified and ranked the highest-value AI applications?

## Key Insight

An organization's weakest dimension usually limits its overall AI progress. Regulated industries (financial services, healthcare, legal) need extra attention to data sovereignty, residency, and compliance requirements.

## Common Failure Mode

The common "pilot purgatory" stall: projects that never scale from proof-of-concept to production. The root cause is typically weak organizational change management and unclear success metrics.

## Recommended Approach

Self-scored checklist (0–5 per dimension) to identify gaps. Total scores guide prioritization of where to invest in building capability.

## Signal for Founders

Enterprise AI readiness frameworks signal a maturing market. Buyers are moving from "should we do AI?" to "where are we weak and how do we fix it?" — creates opportunity for tooling and services targeting specific readiness gaps (data governance, change management, compliance automation).
