# Why Forward-Deployed Engineers Should Build Capability, Not Dependency
# URL: https://cohere.com/blog/forward-deployed-engineers-capability-building
# Date: 2026-08-27
# Source: Cohere Blog
# Author: Nastya Kats

## Summary

Nastya Kats, a Cohere forward-deployed engineer, argues that vendor FDEs should prioritize customer capability-building rather than creating operational dependency.

## Key Arguments

**The FDE Advantage:**
Cohere's FDEs provide deeper product expertise than third-party consultants, offering direct access to engineering teams and faster problem-solving. FDEs can operate across the boundary between deployment and product development, addressing issues at their source rather than through workarounds.

**Addressing Lock-In Concerns:**
While FDEs could theoretically create vendor dependency, Kats distinguishes between commercial dependency (inherent to technology choice) and operational dependency (preventable through knowledge transfer). The latter occurs when "critical knowledge of the system remains with the vendor's engineers."

**Building Independence:**
Cohere's approach emphasizes co-building with customer teams, knowledge transfer sessions, test suite development, and enabling internal "champions." The ultimate goal is customer capability to "operate, evaluate, troubleshoot, and extend the AI system independently."

## Bottom Line

Effective FDE engagements should reduce — not increase — customer reliance on vendor expertise over time, giving enterprises genuine operational control of their AI deployments.
