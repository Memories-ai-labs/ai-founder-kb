# Statement on the US Government Directive to Suspend Access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5

**Title**: Statement on the US government directive to suspend access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5
**Publisher**: Anthropic
**URL**: https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access
**Date**: 2026-06-12
**Source**: Anthropic News

---

The US government issued an export control directive citing national security concerns, requiring Anthropic to suspend all access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for every user, including foreign nationals and foreign employees. The directive arrived at 5:21 PM ET on the day of announcement. All other Anthropic models remain unaffected.

According to the statement, the government believes a method for circumventing Fable 5's safeguards has emerged. After reviewing a demonstration of this technique, Anthropic determined it exposed only minor, previously identified vulnerabilities comparable to weaknesses discoverable in competing publicly available models.

Anthropic outlined its defensive approach: "We have instituted strong safeguards that greatly reduce the likelihood that Fable is misused for tasks related to cybersecurity." The company emphasized that thousands of hours of testing by government agencies, international partners, and third-party organizations validated Fable's safeguard effectiveness relative to existing deployed systems.

The company acknowledged that universal jailbreak resistance may be unachievable currently, with every industry safeguard vulnerable to some form of attack. To address this reality, Anthropic adopted a layered security strategy combining narrow vulnerability constraints, expensive-to-produce exploits, and active monitoring systems. The firm also implemented a 30-day data retention policy specifically to support jailbreak research and mitigation.

Regarding the specific jailbreak identified by officials, Anthropic stated it involved "asking the model to read a specific codebase and fix any software flaws" — a capability already present in competing systems like GPT-5.5.

While complying with the government directive, Anthropic expressed disagreement with the decision, arguing that applying this standard industry-wide would effectively prevent frontier model deployment. The company called for regulatory processes that are transparent, fair, technically grounded, and statutory in nature. Anthropic committed to working toward restoring access and pledged additional disclosure within 24 hours.
