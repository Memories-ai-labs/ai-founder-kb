## Core Arguments

**1. AI sovereignty matters**
AI is becoming core infrastructure for economies, public services, and defense. Countries and institutions should be able to choose systems built under laws they trust — and that they can inspect, adapt, and run in their own environments.

**2. Reject "frontier fatalism"**
The view that only two countries and a few companies can lead AI, and that everyone else should just rent intelligence, amounts to managed decline. Building at the frontier is hard but not limited to one path.

**3. Many domain frontiers exist**
Performance depends on the whole system — context assembly, state tracking, verification, evaluation — not just the base model. This creates space for challengers to compete in specialized domains.

**4. Cybersecurity as proof of concept**
AISLE's results in vulnerability discovery show specialized systems can compete with general-purpose models. Cyber work also offers verifiable ground truth (rare in AI).

**5. Domain work improves general models**
RL on checkable domain problems and real customer feedback loops back into Cohere's general models — specialization and generalization are not opposed.

**6. Airbus analogy**
Europe's aircraft maker broke into an entrenched US market through differentiated approach, patient investment, and partnerships. Same playbook for non-US AI.

## Signal for Founders
Cohere makes the case that "we build, you rent" is not the only model for AI infrastructure. Sovereignty + specialized domains + verifiable ground truth = viable alternative strategy to scale-for-scale's-sake.
