# Cohere and the University of Toronto Partner to Advance Responsible AI Adoption at Scale

*Published July 16, 2026 by Cohere Team*

## Summary

Cohere and the University of Toronto have established a multi-year partnership integrating Cohere's North platform — a "privately deployable agentic AI platform" — into U of T's university-wide enterprise system. Notable because Cohere was co-founded by former U of T students Aidan Gomez, Nick Frosst, and Ivan Zhang (2019).

## Partnership Details

- **Technology:** Cohere's North platform serves as an orchestration layer for "complex task management" across teaching, learning, research, student services, and administrative operations
- **Data control:** Privately deployable — U of T maintains institutional data within its own infrastructure
- **AI Kitchen:** U of T's "secure environment for exploring and evaluating AI tools" will also leverage Cohere technology
- **Canadian AI angle:** Partnership positions as "a Canadian-made, internationally competitive approach to responsible AI"

## Significance

- First major deployment of Cohere's North platform at a university scale
- Demonstrates enterprise AI moving from pilot/experimentation to institution-wide rollout
- Founder-alumni-institution loop: Gomez/Frosst/Zhang founded Cohere after doing research at U of T (Geoffrey Hinton's lab origin)

## Relevance for AI Founders

Enterprise AI adoption pattern: universities are moving from "AI Kitchen" experimentation environments toward institution-wide procurement. The "privately deployable" framing is increasingly the winning enterprise pitch — data sovereignty over raw capability. Cohere's sovereign/private deployment positioning is a direct response to enterprises' reluctance to send sensitive data to public cloud AI APIs.
