# Anthropic Appoints KiYoung Choi as Representative Director of Korea
# URL: https://www.anthropic.com/news/kiyoung-choi-representative-director-anthropic-korea
# Date: 2026-05-26
# source: Anthropic

Anthropic has appointed KiYoung Choi as Representative Director of Korea, ahead of the company's Seoul office opening. According to Anthropic's Economic Index, Korean users engage with Claude at more than 3.5 times the expected rate for the population, with a strong focus on technical and creative applications.

Choi brings over thirty years of technology leadership experience across Korea and the Asia-Pacific region. His previous roles include General Manager at Snowflake for Korea, and country leadership positions at Google Cloud, Adobe, Autodesk, and Microsoft. Throughout his career, he has guided major Korean enterprises through significant technology transitions, including cloud computing and artificial intelligence adoption.

"Korea is one of the most sophisticated AI markets in the world, leading in hardware innovation, developer activity, and enterprise adoption," Choi stated, emphasizing the alignment between Korean organizations' technical capabilities and Anthropic's commitment to responsible AI deployment.

Several Korean companies are already utilizing Claude's capabilities. Law&Company has integrated Claude into its legal assistance platform to help attorneys streamline research and document preparation. SK Telecom, Korea's largest telecommunications provider, selected Claude to develop a custom customer service model that enhances service quality and supports its support teams.

Chris Ciauri, Managing Director of International at Anthropic, highlighted Korea's enthusiasm for Claude and praised Choi's deep understanding of the technology landscape. The Korea team will prioritize partnerships with enterprises and startups, government and research institution engagement, and support for the local developer community.

Career opportunities in Anthropic's Seoul office are available at anthropic.com/careers.
