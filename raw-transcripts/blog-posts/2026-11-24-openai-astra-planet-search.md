# Searching for distant planets with Astra

## Summary

Two amateur astronomers, Kevin Bond and Ethan Byrd, used AI tools to search public telescope data for exoplanet candidates. Bond used Codex; Byrd used Astra. Both have passed their strongest candidates to professional astronomers for review.

## Background

Astronomers have confirmed more than 6,400 exoplanets since 1992. AI has been used in the search for years — in 2017, a neural network helped find an eighth planet around Kepler-90.

## Bond's search

Bond analyzed about 100,000 stars in Kepler data. His program flagged more than 200 signals, including 10 from known planets. Two stood out:

- **KIC 5689121** — a cool star in Lyra with a dip every 13.8 days, possibly a planet about 2.4 times Earth's diameter.
- **KIC 6691030** — a faint northern star with a dip every 4.8 days, consistent with a planet smaller than Neptune.

A SETI Institute astronomer independently found both signals.

## Byrd's search

Byrd analyzed data from about 19 million stars, mainly from NASA's TESS mission. He ran one broad search for candidates and one focused on habitable zones. The first pass produced 49 candidates. One strong candidate is awaiting further analysis by TESS researchers; the search is ongoing.

## Confirming planets

Astronomers must rule out other causes of starlight dips (eclipsing star pairs, instrument temperature changes). Confirmation often requires additional observations (radial velocity measurements) and peer-reviewed publication.

## Signal for AI founders

Demonstrates general-purpose AI agents (Astra, Codex) taking on scientific discovery workflows at citizen-science scale — 19M star dataset analyzed by one person with AI tooling. Signals the democratization of research-grade work via AI agents.
