# Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah's remarks on Pope Leo XIV's encyclical "Magnifica humanitas"
# Author: Chris Olah (Anthropic Co-founder)
# URL: https://www.anthropic.com/news/chris-olah-pope-leo-encyclical
# Date: 2026-05-25
# source: Anthropic News

Chris Olah opened by acknowledging that AI labs operate within incentives that can conflict with doing what's right, noting "pressure to stay commercially viable and to stay at the research frontier. Geopolitical pressure. And the older, plainer pressures of pride and ambition."

He emphasized the critical importance of external oversight, stating that societies need "people outside those incentives—people who care about things going well and insist on safety" to serve as thoughtful critics of AI development.

## Three Key Questions for Discernment

Olah identified three areas where he believes religious and philosophical voices are essential:

**1. Global Equity:** He warned that AI could displace labor at massive scale. The challenge extends beyond supporting displaced workers—AI gains are concentrated in wealthy nations, yet "we do not have a mechanism" to ensure global benefit-sharing.

**2. Human Flourishing:** Olah stressed that questions about how humans, families, and societies should flourish cannot be answered by laboratories alone. These are domains where religious traditions possess millennia of wisdom.

**3. Nature of AI Systems:** Drawing from his research, Olah disclosed that scientists continue discovering mysterious internal structures in AI models, including patterns mirroring neuroscience findings and "internal states that functionally mirror joy, satisfaction, fear, grief, and unease."

He concluded by calling for broader societal engagement—from religious communities to civil society—to thoughtfully guide AI development toward beneficial outcomes.
