# When AI Builds Itself: Our progress toward recursive self-improvement
# URL: https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement
# Date: 2026-06-04
# Source: Anthropic Institute

**Authors:** Marina Favaro and Jack Clark (with editorial support from Santi Ruiz)

## Summary

This article examines Anthropic's progress toward recursive self-improvement—where AI systems design and develop their own successors—and its implications for AI development and society.

### Key Findings

**Evidence of AI Acceleration:**
- Task completion timelines are doubling roughly every four months
- Claude progressed from 4-minute tasks (March 2024) to 12-hour tasks (one year later)
- Public benchmarks show rapid saturation: SWE-bench went from single digits to saturation in two years

**Internal Anthropic Data:**
- Over 80% of merged code is authored by Claude (as of May 2026)
- Engineers now ship 8x more code per quarter compared to 2024 levels
- Claude's success rate on open-ended tasks reached 76% (up 50 percentage points in six months)
- In research tasks, Claude now suggests better next steps than humans 64% of the time

**Human Role Transformation:**
The article notes that as AI handles execution, humans concentrate on direction-setting and judgment. One employee shared: "I started leaning hard into Claudifying about a year ago. That's been crazy and it's now been ~5 months since I last wrote any code myself."

### Three Possible Futures

1. **Trend stalls** but capabilities diffuse widely
2. **Continued acceleration** with humans setting research directions
3. **Full recursive self-improvement** where AI systems build successors autonomously

### Recommendations

Anthropic argues for creating verifiable global coordination mechanisms to enable potential slowdowns or pauses in frontier AI development, allowing society and alignment research to catch up.
