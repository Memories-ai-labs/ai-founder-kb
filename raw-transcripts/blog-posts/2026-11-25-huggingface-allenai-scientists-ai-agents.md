## Study Scope

Ai2 interviewed 17 US-based scientists across natural, applied, and social sciences about how AI agents should be developed responsibly. Exploratory study, not a representative survey.

## Five Dimensions of "Scientific Trust"

1. Validation
2. Privacy
3. Objectivity
4. Accountability
5. Transparency

Scientists need to understand HOW an AI reached an answer, not just whether the answer is correct.

## Key Findings

- **Verification required:** Scientists who delegate tasks must still check outputs against original sources
- **Concerns:** Confident-but-subtly-wrong answers, omitted contradictory evidence, tools that favor preferred hypotheses
- **Proposed evaluations:** 
  - Whether literature-review agents overlook conflicting evidence
  - Whether coding agents access files outside authorized scope
  - Whether data-processing agents match established human workflows
- **Scientific judgment at risk:** Some worry AI use can erode learning
- **Acceptable uses vary:** AI-assisted ideation, writing, manuscript critique — varied by person and field
- **Confidentiality concerns:** Using AI to review unpublished work raised concerns
- **Safeguards cut both ways:** Insufficient safeguards expose sensitive data; overly broad restrictions block legitimate research (e.g., work on political violence, human rights)

## Recommendations

- Scientists should help decide which tasks agents take on and how they're evaluated
- Need training in AI use and enforced guidelines
- Transparent action logs and granular data controls

## Notable Quote

"I don't know what was AI and what was me" — political scientist after rewriting an AI-drafted manuscript
