## Episode Overview

Duration: ~2 hours 22 minutes. Explores how to reconstruct AlphaGo using modern AI tools, examining foundational primitives of machine intelligence: search, learning from experience, and self-play.

"AlphaGo is still the cleanest worked example of the primitives of intelligence: search, learning from experience, and self-play."

## Key Topics

**Game Mechanics & MCTS (0:00–0:32)**
- Go rules and territory capture mechanics
- Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) as solution to Go's massive decision tree
- Action selection using UCB/PUCT criteria balancing exploitation and exploration

**Neural Networks in AlphaGo (0:32–1:00)**
- Policy networks predicting promising moves
- Value networks estimating win probability
- Architecture choices (ResNets vs. Transformers for this domain)

**Self-Play Training (1:00–1:25)**
- How MCTS serves as a policy improvement operator
- Distilling search results back into the network
- Comparison to supervised learning approaches

**Alternative RL Methods (1:25–1:45)**
- Naive policy gradient limitations and high variance problems
- Advantage estimation and credit assignment challenges
- Neural fictitious self-play in complex environments

**LLM Applications (1:45–2:01)**
- Why MCTS doesn't translate directly to language models
- Differences in action spaces and value estimation
- Breadth and depth challenges in reasoning tasks

**Additional Discussion (2:01–2:22)**
- Off-policy training considerations
- Information efficiency in reinforcement learning
- LLM-assisted AI research automation
