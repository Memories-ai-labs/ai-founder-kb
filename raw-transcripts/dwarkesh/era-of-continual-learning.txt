## Overview

Dwarkesh Patel outlines eight predictions for how AI will evolve under continual learning — models that update continuously from user interactions rather than through discrete training-then-deploy cycles. Most current safety regulation and technical alignment work assumes frozen weights; this essay argues the field is not prepared for the paradigm shift.

---

## The Eight Predictions

### 1. Regulatory Approaches Will Need Reimagining

Current safety regulations assume a train-then-deploy model. With continual daily updates, "it would make more sense to do monthly or quarterly risk inspections" rather than focusing on a single pre-deployment checkpoint.

### 2. Technical Alignment Requires New Research

Almost all current alignment techniques are focused on a frozen set of weights. New approaches are needed to prevent jailbreaks and malicious injections when systems continuously learn from multiple users.

### 3. AI Minds Will Become More Diverse

Currently, fewer than five prominent AI systems exist with similar training foundations. Divergent learning experiences across different AI deployments will create genuine diversity in AI "personalities."

### 4. Competitive Advantages Will Compound

Models with larger user bases gain more real-world feedback, creating accelerating returns. "Your competitor who ships earlier might be worse...but it gets to use...more real world experience to get better."

### 5. Pressure for Earlier Deployment

Labs will face incentives to release models sooner to capture learning opportunities. A four-month gap between internal and public release represents forgone improvement data.

### 6. Significant Market Moats Will Emerge

Continual learning creates switching costs absent today. Changing AI providers means "firing an employee that has months of context" and retraining a replacement from scratch.

### 7. Enterprise Lock-in Dynamics

Labs will use incentives and restrictions to encourage data sharing. "The labs may subsidize users" allowing training on sessions while potentially restricting access for those who refuse participation.

### 8. Inference Economies of Scale

Optimal batch sizes for sparse models exceed 2,400 concurrent sequences. Individual users face "100x+ compute efficiency penalties," advantaging large organizations that can sustain personalized weight forks.

---

## Key Implications for Founders

- First-mover advantage in user acquisition becomes much more valuable: more users → more training data → better model → more users (compounding loop)
- Regulatory uncertainty increases; founders building on top of continually-updated models should expect rules to change
- Enterprise sales moat: once a customer's workflows are baked into a continually-trained model, switching costs are enormous
- Locking in safety regulation now (before continual learning is mainstream) is a mistake — the paradigm being regulated doesn't exist yet
