## Key Lessons on Power (from Caro's biographies)

### Robert Moses: Entrenching Power

- **Drafting legislation as power grab:** Moses drafted legislation that expanded his authority beyond what lawmakers intended — technical language locked in advantages
- **Bond covenant lock-in:** Used bond covenants to entrench power, making it contractually difficult to remove him or change the structures he built
- **Populist shield:** Public popularity made him nearly untouchable
- **Federal funds control:** Control over federal funding flows created vast leverage over politicians who needed those funds
- **Beneficiary networks:** Built networks of people who benefited from his power, creating constituencies that defended him
- **Power for its own sake:** Shifted from idealist to someone who valued power intrinsically — Caro compares this transformation to addiction

### LBJ: Manufacturing Power from a Procedural Role

- **Converted a weak position:** Turned Senate majority leader role (historically weak) into genuine influence through procedural mastery
- **Favor economy:** Controlled favors, committee assignments, and Senate agenda
- **Private deal-making:** Brokered private agreements; e.g., maneuvers behind the 1957 Civil Rights Act
- **Information asymmetry:** Knew more about every Senator's needs/vulnerabilities than anyone else

## Signal for Founders/Leaders

Power accumulates through:
1. Technical leverage (code, contracts, structures that are hard to undo)
2. Networks of beneficiaries who defend your position
3. Control over flows (money, favors, information)
4. Converting procedural authority into substantive power

The shift from idealism to power-for-its-own-sake is a well-documented risk for anyone who builds significant power.
