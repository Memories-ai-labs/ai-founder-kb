## Summary

Military historian Si Sheppard discusses how ~600 Spanish soldiers under Cortés conquered the Aztec Empire (6M people) in 2.5 years, and ~190 under Pizarro conquered the Inca Empire (10M people) starting 1532.

## Key Factors Behind Spanish Success

**Military Technology:**
- Steel armor/weapons vs. stone, wood, obsidian
- Horses (psychological shock — unknown in Americas)
- Firearms (limited tactical impact but psychological)
- War dogs

**Strategic / Diplomatic:**
- Capturing supreme leaders destabilized hierarchical societies
- Exploiting existing rivalries (Tlaxcalans vs. Aztecs)
- Coastal retreat bases maintained supply lines
- Playing native groups against each other

**Native Disadvantages:**
- Warfare prioritizing capture for ritual sacrifice vs. elimination
- Agricultural calendar constraints on warfare
- Hierarchical loyalty collapse when leadership fell

## Why Native Forces Couldn't Unite

- Rivalries were centuries-old institutional conflicts, not personal disputes
- Tlaxcalans viewed Aztecs as existential threats regardless of Spanish presence
- No competing European powers to leverage against Spain

## Broader Historical Lessons

- Spain's silver windfall paradoxically weakened it (inflation, no capitalism development)
- Britain's lack of immediate bullion wealth forced productive institutional development
- "Resource curse" pattern: societies with quick resource wealth stagnate (American South with slavery, monocultural colonies)
- China obtained European silver → first truly global trade networks → Ming Dynasty cash crises → Manchu conquest

## Historical Contingency

Sheppard argues conquests weren't inevitable:
- Atahualpa had opportunities to avoid Pizarro's trap
- Manco Inca's siege of Cuzco nearly succeeded
- Different leadership could have changed outcomes
- Long-term Spanish dominance may still have been likely due to disease immunity and tech trajectory gaps
