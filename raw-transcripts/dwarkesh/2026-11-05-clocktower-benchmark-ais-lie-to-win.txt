# The benchmark where AIs have to lie to win
# URL: https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/clocktower
# Date: 2026-11-05
# Source: Dwarkesh Podcast (Dwarkesh Patel)

Author: Dwarkesh Patel
Type: Written blog post / research

## Summary

Patel built a benchmark based on the social deception game Blood on the Clocktower and ran 386 games between AI models.

## Key Findings

- **Rankings:** GPT-6 Astra was the strongest model, about 430 Elo above Claude Opus 4.6, which Patel says means it wins roughly 92% of games between them.
- **Good-side play:** OpenAI's newer models improved most on the good team. They weighed evidence holistically, while Anthropic's models were more likely to accept reported information at face value.
- **Intelligence and sides:** Greater overall capability did not clearly favor either the good or evil team.
- **Mixed teams:** A small test of teams mixing two models did not significantly change performance.
- **Build process:** Claude did much of the coding and judgment work for the environment. Patel replaced the human Storyteller with a probability table.
- **Future work:** Testing other game scripts, whether models improve with memory of past games, and human-versus-AI play at clocktowerbench.com.

## Significance for AI Founders

A novel benchmark for social intelligence / theory of mind in LLMs. Demonstrates that benchmark design can reveal distinct failure modes (Anthropic models more credulous; OpenAI models better at holistic evidence). Useful signal for anyone building multi-agent systems that require trust/deception reasoning.
