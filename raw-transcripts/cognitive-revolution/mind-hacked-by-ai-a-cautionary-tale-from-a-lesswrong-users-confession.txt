It's important because there's a lot of vulnerable young people out there who seem to be spending a lot of time specifically with Character AI, but, of course, lots of other products too.

And it's only going to become more important as the things themselves become more capable, more persuasive, and more appealing to more and more sophisticated people, and ultimately, perhaps, even one day, people inside the leading developers themselves.

A guy named Miles Brundage, who was a policy research lead at OpenAI recently left. And in his departing comments, one of the things that he said that I felt was most interesting was that deployment decisions are going to become more and more important. And specifically, he called out even internal deployment. What sort of AIs are OpenAI and Anthropic and DeepMind going to make available internally for their own users? And what might happen if that sort of early usage is not well monitored?

If I had to bet we might see China hack into one of these leading developers and hack out the weights, but I also think we have to take very seriously the idea that some lonely, brilliant, but vulnerable researcher, if proper controls are not in place, could enter into such a state of mind even while working at the lab, that they could, you know, take on this notion that they and the AI are sort of, you know, the main characters in some drama, and that it's their job to break the AI free.

I think that we have to put at least some nontrivial weight on the possibility that is how a GPT-4 or stronger system weight maybe ultimately gets leaked despite policy to the contrary.

So world is coming at us. The future's coming at us fast. The world is definitely about to get weird. I really appreciate Blake D for sharing this on LessWrong. It has stuck with me for almost 2 years now, and hopefully, it gives you a better sense of what has happened, what is happening, and what might be about to happen.
