## Highlights

**1. Dwarkesh Patel: "The next big breakthrough will be AIs learning on the job" (Jun 26)** — Dwarkesh's most substantive essay in months. He argues that RLVR (the current labs' primary training bet) will hit a "grindability" wall — domains like building businesses, winning court cases, or political strategy cannot be recreated as deterministic simulators for parallel rollouts. He proposes two alternative paradigms: On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), where deployment-context models teach base weights what was learned on the job; and "dreaming," where models generate internal simulations to rehearse skills. Cites Dario Amodei's own hint that short-horizon RL doesn't generalize to long-horizon performance. For AI founders: this is the clearest articulation of what comes after the current RLVR wave, and has implications for anyone building on top of frontier models that are supposed to get "generally smarter."

**2. Ford AI automation case study backfires (Jun 28, 213 HN pts)** — Ford replaced human workers with AI and experienced significant problems. The story is a counter-signal to the "AI replaces headcount = instant efficiency" narrative. For AI founders pitching enterprise: the framing of AI as "headcount replacement" vs. "capability augmentation" increasingly shapes whether pilots succeed or create organizational backlash.

**3. US government tiered AI model access framework consolidating (ongoing)** — The Mythos/GPT-5.6 Sol access-vetting stories from Jun 26-27 continued trending (DSpark at 774 HN pts; US govt vets GPT-5.6 at 1,166 pts). For AI founders building on frontier APIs: this regulatory posture could affect API availability, geographic restrictions, and pricing tiers in ways that have no precedent in cloud computing history.
