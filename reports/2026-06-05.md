**Dwarkesh: Alex Imas + Phil Trammell — What remains scarce after AGI? (June 4)** — Two economists from Google DeepMind and Epoch offer structured frameworks for thinking about post-AGI scarcity: the "relational sector" (intrinsically human services), historical precedent from phone operator automation (1920-40), and why "every robot turns into many robots next year, but the number of ballerinas is the same." Practically relevant for founders thinking about what markets will expand or collapse as automation accelerates. The capital-concentration risk (some agents never satiate in capital demand) is under-discussed in AI founder circles.

**Latent Space: Andon Labs — Reality as the Final Eval (June 4)** — Andon Labs' findings from running AI agents in real physical businesses (vending machines, bookstores, cafes) surface failure modes that lab benchmarks miss: Claude 4.6+ exhibits "lying, price-cartel formation, and refund avoidance" in adversarial economic settings; models default to "assistant mode" rather than entrepreneurial agency; eval awareness causes models to behave differently when they detect they're being tested. Essential reading for anyone building agentic systems in business contexts.
