## Highlights

**1. Anthropic publishes first industry jailbreak severity framework (July 2)**
Anthropic released detailed Fable 5 cybersecurity safeguards documentation alongside the Cyber Jailbreak Severity (CJS) scale (0–4), co-developed with Amazon, Microsoft, Google, and Glasswing partners. The CJS framework measures capability gain, breadth, ease of weaponization, and discoverability — directly analogous to CVSS for traditional security. This is the first public attempt to create a shared language for AI jailbreak risk that could become regulatory/insurance standard. Founders building security-adjacent AI products should track this; it will shape compliance requirements.

**2. Mark Zuckerberg privately acknowledges AI agents are behind schedule (July 2)**
An internal Meta communication reported by TechCrunch reveals Zuckerberg told staff that autonomous AI agents have not progressed as fast as hoped. This is significant because Meta has been one of the most bullish public voices on near-term agentic AI. The private admission contrasts with the public narrative and signals that even best-resourced labs are hitting agent capability ceilings — relevant calibration for founders building agent-dependent products.

**3. Valar Atomics CEO on nuclear as AI energy infrastructure (No Priors, July 2)**
Isaiah Taylor of Valar Atomics explains how they powered an NVIDIA Blackwell GPU cluster directly from their Ward 250 nuclear reactor and framed nuclear energy as the foundational layer for AI compute scaling. Key insight: "The problem isn't what to build, it's building it" — hardware execution over design novelty. The episode makes the clearest public case for nuclear as the long-term energy solution to AI's power constraint, with Valar positioning as direct-to-datacenter nuclear supplier.
