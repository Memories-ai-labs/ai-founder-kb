## Pat Grady + Sonya Huang + Konstantine Buhler (Sequoia) — 2026

**★★★** [Strategy]: The "MAD" framework: Moats (reason from the customer, not from technology — customers change slower than tech) + Affordance (the product has to be self-explanatory the way a hammer is to a two-year-old — Claude Code is powerful but Fortune 500 employees can't open a terminal = affordance gap) + Diffusion gap (frontier capabilities are absorbed far slower than they're produced — that gap is the startup window).

[Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRo33rnv6rQ)

## Pat Grady (Sequoia) — 2026

**★★★** [Strategy]: "AI is a revolution in computation, not communication" — Internet/Cloud/Mobile were all information-distribution revolutions; AI is an information-processing revolution. "The floor keeps moving — no lead is safe, but anybody can win."

[Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRo33rnv6rQ)

## Sonya Huang (Sequoia) — 2026

**★★★** [Strategy]: "Services is the new software" — global software TAM is ~$650B, but legal services in the US alone is $400B. There's a $10T services market for agents to eat. Agentic apps drive 10–40× productivity gains (vs. the prior 10–40%).

[Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRo33rnv6rQ)

## Sonya Huang (Sequoia) — 2026

**★★★** [Engineering & Management]: The agenticness spectrum: tab autocomplete → agentic dev → background / async agents → dark factories (no human review, straight to prod). Bret Taylor rebuilt Sierra over a weekend; Notion rewrote 8M lines in 6 weeks; "what you imagined over 100 years is now possible in 100 days."

[Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRo33rnv6rQ)

## Konstantine Buhler (Sequoia) — 2026

**★★** [Strategy]: The "aluminum/electrolysis" analogy: PhD-level skills are like aluminum in the 1880s (precious). AI = electrolysis, making aluminum dirt cheap. "Skills that took decades to earn are so instantly invoked you can crumple them."

[Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRo33rnv6rQ)
